# Panel R2 — The Advisor-Skeptic: review of the Coder's design (round 2, loop 2)

The Coder has done something rarer than good design: it has done *obedient* design without going limp. Almost everything I demanded is present, verbatim, and frozen. Now I attack what is present.

---

## 1. Binding requirements: verification

**Req 1 — H1 pre-registration in full: MET.** The lexicon is frozen verbatim ("this list may not be edited after the first scoring run"), the index is defined (cᵢ = hᵢ/Tᵢ, pooled C(d) = Σhᵢ/ΣTᵢ), the test is one-sided Jonckheere–Terpstra at α=0.05 plus Spearman with bootstrap CI, drift is Theil–Sen with the threshold defined in words ("drift ≥ 1.0 means the stated deadline recedes at least as fast as time passes"), and the 2027 rubric gives a minimal open-contact definition with success/partial/failure tiers. Two nits, both fixable by text edit: (i) "frozen at this timestamp: design round 2, loop 1" is a *relative* timestamp — pin it to the git commit hash like the ground-truth header does (cff3988); (ii) the JT statistic is ambiguous — is the test on per-claim cᵢ across Δ bins, or on pooled C per bin (which would leave JT with no replicates)? Say it: per-claim cᵢ, bins as ordinal groups, pooled C per bin reported descriptively.

**Req 2 — H4 specification: MET, exceeded.** Exact χ² (Pearson, tag 20 × content_type 4), the V > 0.2 threshold kept, trend model specified (logistic slope on year, one-sided, plus a monotonicity condition), and — beyond my ask — a permutation null within year strata and a video-level Kruskal–Wallis sensitivity to the row-independence assumption. This is the cleanest pre-registration in the document.

**Req 3 — Q3 design document: MET in form, with one downgrade below.** Feature sets (function words/Burrows' Delta, per-register LMs/ΔCE, unmasking), register-control construction (topic-matched pairs, `topic_percentages` as matching index only), the 80–95% role-play band, and the negative-result commitment in writing with four quality gates. The "retraction-level error" clause for origin overreach is exactly right.

**Req 4 — Digest re-verification protocol: MET.** N=8 of 75 (10.7%), seeded, stratified, second model, same prompt verbatim, four agreement metrics with pass/fail thresholds (L1 ≤ 0.15, κ ≥ 0.60, Jaccard ≥ 0.40, prediction match ≥ 75%), fail → downgrade + doubled subsample. This is the protocol I specified, tightened.

**Multiple-comparison policy: MET.** Four primary tests, Holm-corrected; a `report.py` that *refuses to format unbadged p-values* is enforcement by build error, which is the only kind of enforcement I trust; post-hoc null-prior rule present; B7 reports all k ∈ {4,5,6,7}.

## 2. Technical scrutiny

**(a) Conditionality index vs. corpus growth bias: NOT ROBUST AS WRITTEN.** Two distinct problems. First, pooling: "pooled, not averaged, so short claims don't dominate" — true, but it means *long* claims dominate, and later sessions are longer, so C(d) silently re-weights toward the modern era. Report the per-claim median cᵢ alongside pooled C as a sensitivity. Second, the deeper confound nobody in this design names: **Δ is correlated with calendar date.** Claims near their deadline are disproportionately recent, and the doctrine's hedge vocabulary ("timelines", "frequency") plausibly densified over forty years for reasons having nothing to do with deadline proximity. A monotone C vs. Δ trend could be pure era drift. Fix: a within-era sensitivity (JT restricted to post-2015 claims, where the window re-issues are densest) or a per-claim partial Spearman controlling date_made. Without one of these, P1's significant result is uninterpretable — it would ratify hedging-as-era-style, not the ratchet. This is my strongest objection this round.

**(b) JT sample size and power: n IS UNSTATED, and I know roughly why.** The repo's `predictions` block is 29 *year-records*, not 29 dated claims; claims with both a defensible date_made and a stated deadline will be a subset — plausibly 10–20. Worse, P2's drift regression runs on the window re-issues, and the acceptance criterion itself enumerates the chain: 2020 → 2023–33 → 2025–33 → 2026/27 → 2028–29. That is **~5 bounds**. A Theil–Sen slope on five points has a CI you could drive the Mothership through. Ruling: Round 3's README must state the actual n for P1 and P2 before any run — counting rows is metadata inspection, not peeking at outcomes. If n < 10 for either, the honest claim is: "direction consistent/inconsistent with the ratchet, bootstrap CI reported, test underpowered by construction; the ledger is the finding, the statistic is the annotation." A five-point drift slope with CI ≥ 1 is still worth publishing — but as the ledger's velocity, not as inference.

**(c) Stylometry power claim: ASSERTED, NOT COMPUTED. Downgrade.** The sentence "bootstrap power analysis on the role-play baseline band says we detect ΔCE ≥ 0.15 nats/token at ≥80% power" cites no computation, and none exists — the within-class variance of per-document ΔCE for this corpus is unknowable until transcripts land, and the "role-play baseline band" is imported from a different corpus and model family. As written, quality gate (i) of the publishable-negative definition is being *claimed in advance*, which is precisely the sin the gates exist to prevent. Fix: convert §1(c)'s sentence to a "target detectable effect," and pre-register a pilot: after the first 6+6 matched transcripts pass the WER audit, compute the empirical per-document ΔCE variance, run the actual bootstrap power analysis, and only then declare the detectable effect for the full 21+21 run. The full run proceeds regardless; only the power *statement* is conditional.

**(d) The 2027 rubric's "partial" tier: GAMABLE. Tighten.** "Official government acknowledgment of non-human-origin materials... with institutional verification" — *which* institutions? The full-success tier names its bar (≥2 major scientific institutions); the partial tier doesn't. A believer will score a congressional hearing, or a Pentagon "materials of unknown origin" remark, as partial. And "mass-witnessed, multi-instrument-recorded... endorsed as anomalous by ≥2 national space/defense agencies" is three undefined thresholds stacked. Tighten: partial requires (i) the official statement to use non-human-origin language explicitly, not "unknown"; (ii) verification by a named class — national laboratory or peer-reviewed analysis; (iii) mass-witnessed defined numerically (say ≥1,000 independent witnesses) and multi-instrument as ≥2 independent instrument classes (radar + optical, not two cameras). What I will defend: the 2030-01-01 deadline and the "retroactive reinterpretation does not score" clause. Disentangling outcome from window movement is correct — the slip is logged as a ratchet event, the rubric stays blind to timing. That part is well built.

**(e) Whisper WER gates on 1984–96 cassette audio: OPTIMISTIC, but the failure mode is managed — barely.** Whisper large-v3 hits ~3% WER on clean read speech; on cassette-sourced, reverberant, fast trance-register audio with audience bleed, 15% overall is a hope, not a gate, and function-word WER ≤ 10% is *harder*, not easier — short unstressed function words are the first casualty of hiss, and they are the load-bearing subset for F1. Two additions required: (i) a Whisper **hallucination screen** — Whisper inserts fluent invented text into low-signal spans, which is catastrophic for stylometry; flag spans with low audio energy and high text output, and exclude them; (ii) the fallback ("runs only on spans passing the gate") induces **selection bias** — clean spans are quieter rooms and better microphones, so era and audio quality become confounded; the era-extension arm may degrade to descriptive. Say so now, in the design, so nobody rescues a Frontier grade later.

## 3. Ruling on Round 3: implementation may start, conditionally.

Two text edits first — absolute timestamp on the frozen lexicon, and the JT-statistic clarification. Neither requires re-analysis; both are cheaper now than after code exists. Priority order:

1. **acquisition.py (B5) — immediately.** Step zero as ruled; every week of delay is pure loss, and the WER audit plus the 6+6 pilot now gates the stylometry power statement.
2. **demand.py (P3/P4).** Cheapest, cleanest, fully in-repo; ship it first as the working proof of the badging/Holm machinery.
3. **predictions.py (P1/P2)** — with the era-confound sensitivity and the stated-n requirement.
4. **doctrine_graph.py** — descriptive only, no origin language, as the design already concedes.
5. **stylometry.py — blocked** until WER audit passes and the pilot-based power computation lands.

## 4. Goal check

Goal v2 survives design intact — one clause needs updating. "Blocked on raw-transcript acquisition, which is therefore step zero" is no longer aspirational: acquisition is now pre-registered with quality gates, a hallucination screen (per this round), and a pilot-power contingency. Amend v2's third sentence to: *"Pre-registered as step zero, with ASR quality gates: whether the channeled persona is computationally distinguishable from its human author..."* Everything else — "best-documented," the quietism, the honesty standard — holds. The design honors the amended goal; the goal now describes the design.

The Coder has earned its round. The era confound in (a) is the one place where this panel could still fool itself, and I want it fixed in text before a single figure is drawn.
