## A. Free vibration of an elastic sphere (Lamb problem)

**On the Vibrations of an Elastic Sphere** — Horace Lamb, *Proc. London Math. Soc.* s1-13 (issue dated Nov. 1881, vol. for 1881–82), pp. 189–212, doi:10.1112/plms/s1-13.1.189 (open PDF: https://zenodo.org/records/2423349) — Original exact solution of free vibrations of a homogeneous isotropic sphere; introduces the two mode families (modes "of the first class" = torsional/toroidal, divergence-free; "second class" = spheroidal) with eigenfrequencies as roots of transcendental equations in spherical Bessel functions; torsional roots depend only on c_T, spheroidal roots on c_T/c_L (Poisson's ratio). [verified: fetched]

**What can we learn about GW physics with an elastic spherical antenna?** — J. A. Lobo, *Phys. Rev. D* 52, 591 (1995), doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.52.591; arXiv:gr-qc/0006102 — Gives explicit tables of dimensionless eigenvalues kR = ωR/c_T. Spheroidal (σ = 0.33): l=0: 5.4322, 12.138, 18.492…; l=1: 3.5895, 7.2306, 8.4906…; l=2: **2.6497**, 5.0878, 8.6168…. Toroidal (independent of σ): l=1: **5.7635**, 9.0950, 12.323…; l=2: **2.5011**, 7.1360, 10.515…. [verified: fetched]

**Longitudinal versus transverse spheroidal vibrational modes of an elastic sphere** — L. Saviot, D. B. Murray, *Phys. Rev. B* 72, 205433 (2005), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.72.205433; arXiv:cond-mat/0506353 — Defines η = ωR/v_T and ξ = ωR/v_L, relates v_T/v_L = √[(1−2ν)/(2−2ν)]; Table II (v_T/v_L = 0.5, i.e. ν = 1/3), lowest spheroidal modes: l=0: 5.49; l=1: 3.60; l=2: 2.65; l=3: 3.95; l=4: 5.07. Shows "SPH_T" modes (incl. the l=2 football mode) are nearly independent of v_L, i.e. weakly ν-dependent. Cites Lamb 1882 and Eringen & Suhubi for the boundary determinant. [verified: fetched]

**Lamb-mode calculator for a free elastic sphere** — L. Saviot, https://saviot.cnrs.fr/lamb/index.en.html — In-browser solver: inputs v_L, v_T (or ν), diameter, spheroidal/torsional, l (0–8), overtone n; outputs f, η = ωR/v_T, ξ = ωR/v_L and mode shapes; each mode (2l+1)-fold degenerate. Cites Lamb (1881) and Eringen & Suhubi vol. II pp. 804–833 and warns that the l=0 spheroidal calculation in that book is incorrect. [verified: fetched]

**Elastodynamics, Vol. II: Linear Theory** — A. C. Eringen, E. S. Suhubi, Academic Press, New York, 1975 (sphere vibrations pp. 804–833) — Standard textbook derivation of the Lamb frequency equations (displacement/stress expressions used by Saviot et al.); note the l=0 error flagged above. [verified: search snippet]

**Basic study on the oscillation of a homogeneous elastic sphere (Parts I–III)** — Y. Satô, T. Usami, *Geophys. Mag.* 31, 15 (1962) (continued pp. 25, 49) — Classical numerical tables/plots of spheroidal and torsional eigenfrequencies of a homogeneous sphere vs Poisson's ratio (seismological context); cited by Saviot et al. as the reference computation. GSJ catalogue was under maintenance; citation taken from the fetched Saviot et al. reference list. [verified: search snippet]

**Novel Method of Measuring Elastic and Anelastic Properties of Solids** — D. B. Fraser, R. C. LeCraw, *Rev. Sci. Instrum.* 35, 1113 (1964) — First "resonant sphere" measurement: used Lamb's analytic solution for an isotropic sphere and inverted it graphically for the elastic constants; described as possibly the first RUS measurement (Maynard 1996; Wikipedia RUS). [verified: search snippet]

**Elastic Constants and Their Measurement** — E. Schreiber, O. L. Anderson, N. Soga, McGraw-Hill, New York, 1973 (©1973, printed 1974), xi+196 pp., ISBN 0-07-055603-2 — Monograph covering the resonant sphere technique (RST) and RPR methods incl. tables for sphere mode identification; cited by the Migliori sphericity patent for resonant-sphere methods. [verified: fetched (NYPL catalogue record)]

**On the Determination of Elastic and Anelastic Properties of Isotropic Spheres** — M. L. Smith, US Army CRREL Research Report 299, March 1972, https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD0741355.pdf — Theory of free/forced oscillations of (layered) spheres, Rayleigh-principle perturbations, stochastic inversion of frequencies for v_P, v_S; experiments on 4-in. Lucite and fused-quartz spheres; inversion r.m.s. relative error 0.09 % (Lucite, 0S2 omitted) and ≈0.03 % (fused quartz); usable down to Q ≈ 100; estimates transducer/support loading shifts. [verified: fetched]

**Elastic constants and anelastic properties of an anisotropic periclase sphere as determined by the resonant sphere technique** — H. Oda, S. Isoda, Y. Inouye, I. Suzuki, *J. Geophys. Res.* 99 (1994), doi:10.1029/94JB01017 — Measured the lowest 22 resonances (1.0–2.6 MHz) and their attenuation of a single-crystal MgO sphere, identified modes against computed spectra, inverted for elastic constants and internal friction; claims up to 10× better accuracy than RPR. [verified: fetched (EarthRef record)]

**Simultaneous determination of elastic constants and asphericity of aspherical specimen by the resonant sphere technique** — H. Oda, 131st ASA Meeting, Indianapolis, May 1996, abstract 5aPA6 — Linearised least-squares fit of frequency shifts to shape asphericity plus elastic-constant corrections (olivine). [verified: fetched]

**The resonant ultrasound spectroscopy method for determining the Poisson's ratio of spheres over the full range** — D. Li, L. Dong, R. S. Lakes, *Materials Letters* 143, 31–34 (2015) (preprint https://lakeslab.ep.wisc.edu/RUSspheresPreprint.pdf) — FEM maps of sphere mode frequencies vs ν ∈ (−1, 0.5); fundamental is 1T2 at f/f₀ = 2.5 with f₀ = (1/2R)√(G/πρ) for −0.13 < ν < 0.48; 1S2 is the 3rd mode for ν > −0.13; torsional modes insensitive to ν; slight (<5 %) mode splitting from anisotropy; ovality shifts most modes down but 1T2 and 2S1 are insensitive. [verified: fetched]

**Sphericity determination using resonant ultrasound spectroscopy** — US Patent 5,355,731 (Regents of Univ. of California; Migliori et al.), issued 18 Oct 1994 — Each sphere mode is (2l+1)-fold degenerate; ellipsoidal distortion lifts the degeneracy fully, axisymmetric distortion partially; splitting is linear in the small shape deviation. Uses the universal reduced-frequency-vs-ν plot (valid 0.25 ≤ ν ≤ 0.35) to identify nSl / nTl modes; quotes 1T2 reduced frequency **2.5011**, lowest T2 member shifts −0.93 % per +1 % in (d₃₊ + d₃₋). [verified: fetched]

**A new closed-form model for isotropic elastic sphere including new solutions for the free vibrations problem** — E. Hanukah, arXiv:1311.0741 — Polynomial (order-1…4) closed-form approximations; FEM reference at ν = 0.3 gives lowest 5-fold mode ω̄ = 1.551 and next 5-fold mode ω̄ = 1.641 with ω̄ = ωR/√(E/ρ) *(derived: multiplying by √(2(1+ν)) gives ωR/c_T = 2.50 and 2.65, i.e. the T2 and S2 multiplets)*; first-order estimate ω̄² = 5/(1+ν) for the shear quintet. [verified: fetched]

**Far-infrared and Raman vibrational transitions of a solid sphere: Selection rules** — E. Duval, *Phys. Rev. B* 46, 5795 (1992) — Group-theoretical selection rules for Lamb modes (which (l, spheroidal/torsional) modes couple to which probes). [verified: search snippet]

*Derived numbers for the Cu ball (using G_Hill ≈ 47.3 GPa, ρ = 8930 kg m⁻³ → c_T ≈ 2300 m s⁻¹, c_L ≈ 4740 m s⁻¹):* f(1T2) ≈ 72 kHz (5-fold), f(1S2) ≈ 76 kHz (5-fold), f(1S1) ≈ 103 kHz, f(1S0) ≈ 156 kHz, f(1T1) ≈ 166 kHz; expect each multiplet split by anisotropy/asphericity at the 10⁻³–10⁻² level.

**The Theory of Sound** (Rayleigh's principle) — J. W. S. [verified: fetched (catalogue records)]

*Design note:* the 68 g ball placed at point A on the table acts as a point mass (below the Hertz bounce frequency, see E) and will lower the table-plate modes according to Mace/Rayleigh by ≈ −½·(68 g / modal mass)·φ²(A); the table's spectrum therefore legitimately depends on A vs B, whereas the ball's own elastic spectrum should not.

## General principle: a free body's natural frequencies depend only on geometry, material and boundary conditions

1. **Lamb 1882** (A) — the eigenvalue equations contain only R, ρ, λ, μ (through c_T, c_L): no position-dependent quantity appears. [verified: fetched]
2. **Rayleigh, *The Theory of Sound*, Vol. 1, Ch. IV–V** (Dover 1945, ISBN 0-486-60292-3) — normal modes and natural frequencies are determined by the system's inertia and stiffness (T and V quadratic forms); Rayleigh's principle for the effect of added mass/stiffness. [verified: fetched (catalogue)]
3. **Landau & Lifshitz, *Theory of Elasticity*, 3rd ed.** (Pergamon, 1986; §22 Elastic waves in an isotropic medium p. 87, §23 Elastic waves in crystals p. 92, §24 Surface waves p. 94, §25 Vibration of rods and plates p. 99) — eigenfrequencies of a finite body follow from the elastodynamic equation with free-surface boundary conditions and scale as c/L (the §22 problems treat the radial oscillations of a sphere). [verified: fetched (HathiTrust record + table of contents; §22 problem text not opened)]
4. **K. F. Graff, *Wave Motion in Elastic Solids*** (Ohio State Univ. Press / OUP 1975, 649 pp.; Dover reprint 1991, ISBN 0-486-66745-6) — waves and vibrations in strings, rods, membranes, plates, shells and infinite/semi-infinite media; frequencies set by geometry and wave speeds. [verified: fetched (TRID record)]
5. **Avitabile 2001** (D) — "natural frequencies, damping and mode shapes are the structure's dynamic properties … independent of the applied force", and depend on the structure's weight and stiffness, with the caveat that the supports and transducers are part of the structure under test. [verified: fetched]
6. **G. Liu, Navier–Lamé spectral invariants** (G) — the elastic spectrum is a functional of the domain and the Lamé constants alone; it determines |Ω| and |∂Ω| and singles out the ball. [verified: fetched]

**Implication for the experiment:** nothing in the ball's (or shell's) elastic eigenproblem depends on where the body sits; the only physical channels by which "location" can enter are (i) temperature differences (≈ −200 ppm K⁻¹), (ii) the support (Hertz contact stiffness ∝ g^{1/3}, affecting only the ~1.4 kHz bounce mode at the 10⁻⁹ cm⁻¹ level, and the slightly different coupling/damping to the table), (iii) air loading (density/temperature-dependent radiation damping, ~10⁻⁵ level for a metal ball in air), (iv) the ambient magnetic field for any NMR-type "resonance" of ⁶³Cu (≈565 Hz in 50 µT, shifting ~11 Hz per µT), and (v) relativistic effects (gravitational redshift 1.1×10⁻¹⁶ m⁻¹) that are many orders below any RUS resolution (ppm). The table's own spectrum, by contrast, must change between A and B because the ball is an added point mass at a location-dependent mode-shape amplitude.

### I.1 Free vibration of a complete thin elastic spherical shell

**On the Vibrations of a Spherical Shell** — H. Lamb, *Proc. London Math. Soc.* s1-14 (Nov. 1882), pp. 50–56, doi:10.1112/plms/s1-14.1.50 — Lamb's thin-shell (membrane) limit of the sphere problem: two classes of modes; closed-form results reproduced by Clougherty & Gorman (below): torsional ("shear") modes Ω_T = ωR/c_T = √((l−1)(l+2)) (l = 2: 2.000; l = 3: 3.162), breathing mode ωR/c_T = 2√γ with γ = (1+ν)/(1−ν), l = 1 upper mode ωR/c_T = √(6γ); each mixed (spheroidal) l ≥ 2 has two branches. [verified: fetched (OUP issue record); content via fetched secondary source]

**On the low-frequency vibrational modes of C₆₀** — D. P. Clougherty, J. P. Gorman, arXiv:mtrl-th/9604002 (1995) — Summarises Lamb's thin-shell results in closed form (shear modes Ω = √((l−1)(l+2)); breathing 2√γ; l = 1: √(6γ); l ≥ 2 mixed modes two branches), with Ω = ωR/v_s; notes ω(M₁)/ω(M₀) = √(3/2) independent of elastic constants. [verified: fetched]

**Axisymmetric Modes of Vibration of Thin Spherical Shell** — W. E. Baker, *J. Acoust. Soc. Am.* 33(12), 1749–1758 (Dec. 1961) — Membrane-theory frequency equation for the complete shell. In the form requested, with λ_n = n(n+1), Ω = ωR/c_p, c_p² = E/[ρ(1−ν²)]:
  Ω⁴ − Ω²[λ_n + 1 + 3ν] + (1−ν²)(λ_n − 2) = 0
Checks: n = 0 ⇒ Ω² = 2(1+ν) ⇒ **ω₀ = (1/R)√(2E/[ρ(1−ν)])** (breathing; the other root is spurious); n = 1 ⇒ Ω² = 0 (rigid translation) or 3(1+ν); n → ∞ lower branch ⇒ Ω² → (1−ν²), i.e. ω → √(E/ρ)/R (the membrane "lower-branch limit"). I confirmed this equation numerically against the fetched Abaqus benchmark table (which is computed from Baker): with E = 180 GPa, ρ = 7670 kg m⁻³, ν = 1/3 and R = 3.0 m it reproduces 445.0 Hz (n = 0), 545.18 Hz (n = 1 upper), 187.34 / 748.02 Hz (n = 2 lower/upper) to < 0.1 %. ### I.2 Point-load stiffness of a spherical shell (Reissner)

**Stresses and small displacements of shallow spherical shells, I and II** — E. Reissner, *J. Math. Phys.* (MIT) 25, 80–85 (1946) and 25, 279–300 (1947) — Shallow-shell theory; solution for a concentrated normal load gives the deflection under the load
  w₀ = √(3(1−ν²)) · P R / (4 E h²)   ⇔   k = P/w₀ = 4 E h² / [R √(3(1−ν²))] ≈ 2.42 E h²/R (ν = 0.3).
This coefficient follows from the shallow-shell Green's function w(0) = P/(8Dλ²) with D = Eh³/12(1−ν²), λ⁴ = Eh/(R²D) *(my derivation)*, and it is exactly Vella et al.'s k₁ = 8√(B·Et·κ_G) with κ_G = 1/R² (below). The form printed in Reissner's paper itself was not seen. [verified: search snippet (citation as printed in fetched Vella et al. 2012: "J. Math. Phys. 25, 80 (1947); 25, 279 (1947)")]

Woinowsky-Krieger, McGraw-Hill, 1959 — Art. 132 "Symmetrical bending of shallow spherical shells", p. 558 ff (based on Reissner), gives the apex deflection under a concentrated load; Art. 111 "Spherical shell supported at isolated points", p. 453. The formula text was not reached in the fetched copy (TOC only). [verified: fetched (TOC)]
