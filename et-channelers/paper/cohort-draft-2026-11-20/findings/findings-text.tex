% Finding 1. Source: descriptives-summary.json icc_between_creators; senior-analyst-pilot.json variance_partition_3_level.
\paragraph{Recordings of one creator resemble each other.}
At the snapshot of \SnapDate{} (\SnapLines{} finished transcripts of \NRunAudio{} audio files; provisional), the creator accounts for \IccMinPct\% to \IccMaxPct\% of the variance of seven recording-level measures (Table~\ref{tab:variance}, Figure~\ref{fig:variance}): lexical variety \IccMattr, pace \IccPace, share of time without speech \IccNonSp, mean log-probability \IccLogp, length \IccLen, question share \IccQuest{} and repeated five-word share \IccRep. For pace, the \IccPaceRecordings{} recordings carry the information of about \NeffPace{} independent ones. Restricted to the \SnapCreatorsFivePlus{} creators with five or more transcripts, the estimate for pace is \IccPaceSeen{} with a 95\% bootstrap interval of \IccPaceSeenLo{} to \IccPaceSeenHi; the intervals for share without speech (\IccNonSpSeenLo{} to \IccNonSpSeenHi) and for log-probability (\IccLogpSeenLo{} to \IccLogpSeenHi) are wide, so those two shares are poorly determined. In a pilot on \VpLedger{} transcripts, the variance of articulation rate splits into \VpArtCreator{} between creators, \VpArtRecording{} between recordings of one creator and \VpArtWithin{} within a recording. The middle 80\% of creator medians of pace lie between \CrPacePten{} and \CrPacePninety{} words per minute of speech (10th and 90th percentiles; Figure~\ref{fig:pace}). The unit of inference is therefore the creator, of which there are about one hundred.

% Finding 2. Source: descriptives-summary.json quantiles and length_bands_minutes; words from the per-recording CSV.
\paragraph{The recordings differ widely in form.}
The median recording lasts \LenMed{} minutes (quartiles \LenQi{} and \LenQiii; maximum \LenMax) and holds \WordsMed{} words; \NLenUpToFive{} recordings are five minutes or shorter and \NLenOverSixty{} are longer than an hour. The median pace of English recordings is \PaceMed{} words per minute of speech (quartiles \PaceQi{} and \PaceQiii; Table~\ref{tab:descriptives}, Figure~\ref{fig:distributions}).

% Finding 3. Source: descriptives-summary.json nonspeech_hours, quality_flags, quiet stretches, timeline_profile_by_tenth;
% the top-five share is recomputed from the per-recording CSV and agrees with the audit (44%).
\paragraph{About one eighth of the audio holds no speech.}
Of \SnapHours{} audio hours, \SnapNonSpeechHours{} (\SnapNonSpeechPct\%) hold no speech according to the recognizer's voice detector, and five creators hold \NonSpTopFivePct\% of that time. \NHalfNonSpeech{} recordings are at least half without speech and \NUnderFiftyWords{} hold fewer than 50 words. Stretches of 30 seconds or more without speech total \QuietHours{} hours in \NQuietRecordings{} recordings. Speech is thinner at the ends of a recording: for the \SnapCreatorsFivePlus{} creators with five or more transcripts, weighted equally, it covers \CovSeenFirst{} of the first tenth of the running time, \CovSeenMiddle{} in the middle and \CovSeenLast{} of the last tenth (Figure~\ref{fig:running}).

\paragraph{Recognizer confidence follows delivery.}
Across \RhoN{} English recordings the mean segment log-probability rises with pace (Spearman \RhoLogpPace) and falls with the share of time without speech (\RhoLogpNonSp); it is unrelated to length (\RhoLogpLen). Across the \SnapCreatorsFivePlus{} creator medians the correlation with pace is \RhoCreatorLogpPace. Inside a recording the picture differs: in a pilot on \RhoWithinRecordings{} recordings the correlation between segment log-probability and segment pace has a median of \RhoWithinMed{} and is positive in \RhoWithinPositivePct\% of recordings. The share of segments below the recognizer's stock cut-off of \StockLogpCutoff{} is \ShareBelowCutoffMed{} at the median and \ShareBelowCutoffPninetyfive{} at the 95th percentile, so the cut-off flags almost nothing. We treat log-probability as a covariate and not as a quality score until it has been calibrated against hand transcripts.

\paragraph{Recognizer artefacts are frequent and tied to the decoding window.}
In \ArtEnglishBase{} English transcripts a two-word thanks phrase stands alone as a segment \ThanksSegments{} times, in \ThanksTranscripts{} transcripts (\ThanksPct\%). \ThanksLateWindowPct\% of these segments start 27 to 30 seconds into a \WindowSeconds-second decoding window and \ThanksEmbeddedPct\% sit in running speech with under a second of gap on both sides; their median log-probability is \ThanksLogp{} against \OtherShortLogp{} for other short segments. A further \IsoSegments{} isolated segments of at most three words sit in silence or music in \IsoTranscripts{} transcripts (\IsoPct\%), \IsoGridPct\% of them starting exactly on a multiple of 30 seconds (Table~\ref{tab:artefacts}). In the reading sample a single name was spelt in \SpellingsMin{} to \SpellingsMax{} ways inside one recording. These counts come from pattern rules that have not been checked against listening.

% Finding 6. Source: transcript-reading-notes.md 0.1 and 0.5; question share from descriptives-summary.json.
\paragraph{A recording filed under a channeler is often not channelled speech.}
Of \ReadLongN{} read recordings with 300 words or more, \KindChannelled{} hold a channelled voice or a relayed message; the others are interviews or podcasts without channelling (\KindInterview), guided meditations (\KindMeditation), talks in the creator's own voice (\KindOwnVoice), and \KindOther{} recordings of other kinds (Table~\ref{tab:kinds}). Only \ReadAudienceQA{} of the \ReadN{} read recordings hold audience questions and answers, so the share of segments that end in a question mark (median \QuestMed) does not measure audience questions. The sample was chosen for contrast and coded by one reader: it shows that these kinds exist, not how common they are.

% Finding 7. Source: transcript-reading-notes.md 0.2 (also catalogue 2.3). Crude patterns: an order of magnitude.
\paragraph{Extraterrestrial vocabulary is a minority register.}
Among \TermBase{} English transcripts of 150 words or more, \TermSoul\% contain a soul term, \TermFrequency\% a frequency or vibration term, \TermBreath\% a breath term and \TermAscension\% an ascension term, against \TermExtraterrestrial\% with any extraterrestrial or alien word. The patterns are crude and unvalidated.

\paragraph{The newest \NPerCreator{} recordings are a quota, not a period.}
The window covered by a creator's newest \NPerCreator{} recordings spans \SpanMin{} to \SpanMax{} days (median \SpanMed; quartiles \SpanQi{} and \SpanQiii): \NSpanWithinMonth{} creators fit inside 30 days and \NSpanOverTwoYears{} need more than two years (Figure~\ref{fig:calendar}). \PctDatedCaptureYear\% of the \NSelected{} selected recordings are dated in the year of capture and \PctDatedYearBefore\% in the year before. \NExactDates{} dates are exact days and \NEstimatedDates{} are intervals estimated from a displayed age (median width \EstimatedDateMedianWidth{} days). Order within a creator is not time across creators, and upload date is not the date of speaking, so the cohort supports no trend claim.

\paragraph{Language and recognizer model.}
At the snapshot, \SnapNonEnglish{} audio files from \SnapNonEnglishCreators{} creators are in a language other than English (Japanese \SnapJapanese, Spanish \SnapSpanish, Portuguese \SnapPortuguese, Czech \SnapCzech), so a language here means one or two creators. English speech was transcribed by a distilled English model and other languages by the full multilingual model (\NModelDistilEnglish{} and \NModelFull{} files among the \ModelCountBase{} finished when the count was made). No comparison crosses the two models until both have been run on the same audio.

% E. DRAFT TEXT: what is not yet known

\paragraph{What is not yet known.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Recognition error.} No word error rate has been measured on any stratum and no reference transcript exists. All \SnapLines{} transcripts are unreviewed, and no recording has a language confirmed by a person. Word time stamps and the split into speech and non-speech are model output.
\item \emph{Speakers.} Speaker separation has not been run. Host, guest, audience, the creator's own voice and a channelled voice are one stream, and no number is per channelled word.
\item \emph{Experiments.} The \NExperiments{} experiments and \NRules{} rules are a design. What exists is a descriptive pass, three pattern passes, one reading by one reader and pilots whose code was not reviewed.
\item \emph{Registration.} No registry of hypotheses is frozen, no confirmatory test has been made and no p-value is reported. The \NHoldout{} hold-out creators have not been analysed.
\item \emph{Comparison.} No corpus of non-channelling speech has been collected, so nothing here can be called distinctive of channelling. The replication wave has not been collected either.
\item \emph{Bridge.} The two recognizer models have not been run on the same audio.
\item \emph{Completeness.} The snapshot covers \SnapPctAudio\% of the audio files and \SnapPctHours\% of the hours of the run, and the remainder is not random: of \SnapNotInLedger{} recordings then outstanding, \SnapNotInLedgerConcentrated{} belong to \SnapNotInLedgerCreators{} creators. \SnapHeld{} recordings were held for a decision. \NOtherText{} recordings held as other kinds of text and \NFailed{} without audio are outside the run.
\item \emph{Artefacts.} No artefact filter has been applied to any number reported here, and the precision and recall of the removal rules are unmeasured.
\item \emph{Kinds of recording.} The kinds rest on one reader and a purposive sample; there are no segment-level labels and no second coder.
\item \emph{Intervals.} The coverage audit by simulation has not been run; the intervals shown are plain percentile bootstrap intervals.
\end{itemize}
