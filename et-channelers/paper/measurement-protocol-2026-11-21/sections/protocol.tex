\section{An internally registered, then amended, protocol for model-coded labels}
\label{sec:protocol}

OpenAI language models coded every label with frozen prompts and no person checked any, so agreement between models
speaks to reliability, not validity \cite{pangakis2024keeping}; Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} holds the full record.

\subsection{Registry, hold-out and freezes}
\label{sec:registry}

The registry of analyses on data already collected \cite{vandenakker2021preregistration,weston2019recommendations}
was frozen at \RegistryFrozenStamp: ten hypotheses (Table~\ref{tab:hypotheses}) under Holm's step-down correction
over ten places \cite{holm1979simple}, and thirteen exploratory questions. An exploratory reporting plan, registered by
hash after most of its outputs were built, reports each one whatever it shows; its round's outputs are in the
exploratory release (Section~\ref{sec:results}). The registry is internally registered: commits and
a SHA-256 hash fix its content, but without an external, time-stamped deposit \cite{nosek2018preregistration} they
prove content, not time.

\paragraph{Hold-out.}\label{sec:holdout}
The hold-out, a split sample of whole speakers \cite{anderson2017splitsample,egami2022make}, is the \NHoldout{}
creators with at most three of the first \SnapLines{} finished transcripts and ten or more to come: the tail of an
English-first run queue, holding all \NNonEnglishCreators{} creators with a non-English transcript. The split is not
random: fields known before the run predict membership (cross-validated AUC \DTwoAuc; 95\% shuffle band
\DTwoAucBandLo--\DTwoAucBandHi; Figure~\ref{fig:love}), so hold-out estimates are never read against seen ones as
replication.

\paragraph{Freezes.}\label{sec:freezes}
The instrument freeze (freeze 1, \FreezeOneStamp) hashed \FreezeOneFiles{} files and the configuration, including
the models (small \ModelSmall, middle \ModelMiddle, large \ModelLarge), and unlocked seen labels only. The analysis
freeze (freeze 2, \FreezeTwoStamp) hashed \FreezeTwoModules{} analysis modules and the constants computed on seen
labels, and named the registry commit holding A10; no hold-out label existed before it.

\subsection{What the instruments code, and their gates}
\label{sec:instruments}

The labelling unit is a transcript sentence. Voice is the creator's own, claimed, or other speakers'; claimed
voice joins the first-person claimed voice, coded embodied (the creator speaks in the first person as a named
non-human source), and the reported claimed voice, coded relayed (the creator reports what a source is said to say). Asks are to pay or enrol, to engage on the
platform, or to move off it. Forecasts of record carry five checkability parts and a commitment code; sentence codes
group into world claim, practice and feeling, or neither; title codes give a title's presentation and the family of
sources named.

Gates on the gate sample, which holds development creators and every check creator, test valid answers (A), exact quotations (B), stability over three runs (C),
two-model agreement (D) and agreement with a second finder (E); each passes when its point estimate reaches the floor
and its lower 90\% bound the floor minus 0.10 (Table~\ref{tab:gates}). Three things weaken a pass, above all one at the
floor (voice stability \VoiceGateC{} against \VoiceGateCFloor): gate D's two models share a vendor, and model errors
correlate \cite{kim2025correlated}; the bounds' coverage was not audited; and instruments were revised and re-gated on
the same samples.

Five choices followed a gate result on the seen gate samples, all before any hold-out label (directions in
Table~\ref{tab:deviations}).
(i)~The owner kept checkability (H2) and the title codes (H5, H9) below their floors on the seen gate samples, with
large-model adjudication (the gate sample and the hold-out reliability sample drew harder titles than the rest of the
seen roster; outside them, presentation agreement was higher); A10's gate rule (A10.5), entered later, wrote this exception in and extends it to no other
instrument. (ii)~Relayed voice and the pre-declared merge failed gate D, so by the rule's letter H1, H4 and H6 would
not have run; the lead merged relayed into claimed voice for H1 and H6, H4 keeping embodied voice. The lead also
(iii)~moved the ask check's record (the model whose labels the tests read) to the middle model after the small model failed exact quotation, (iv)~made
``forecast of record or not'' the class of record after the ten-class label failed stability and (v)~froze the
instruments before the last known-answer batches.

The voice record is the small model on seen creators and the middle model on the hold-out, so labeller and group are
confounded in H4's yardstick (the between-creator SD that scales its drops), set from small-model seen labels. The voice label also inherits the recognizer: it moves
more without punctuation and case than between runs (\VoicePunctMovePct\% against \VoiceRunMovePct\% of words).

On the hold-out reliability sample (R-hold: \RholdRecordings{} hold-out recordings drawn from run-ledger fields only,
after the analysis freeze), voice (flagged on its valid-answer gate), asks and commitment passed, checkability and the
title codes stayed below their floors under the owner's exception (R-hold, like the gate sample, drew harder titles
than the rest of the seen roster; outside them, presentation agreement was higher), and two instruments failed a
gate (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Amendments and the guard}
\label{sec:amendments}

The registry holds \NAmendments{} amendments: A1 to A4 from its freeze, A5 to A11 after H3's result
(Section~\ref{sec:hthree}); A6 and A9 drop H3 and H8 ($p = 1$). A10 (decided before the instrument freeze, worded
after it without using any label, entered from a hashed draft before the analysis freeze) puts a creator-level
sign-flip $p$ (an exact permutation of creator values' signs) into Holm with $m = 10$, unrun places at $p = 1$.
It adds ``not determinable: too few eligible creators'', conditional estimators for H5 and H6, the combined gate rule
and Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman intervals \cite{inthout2014hartung}. Where A10 replaces an estimator, reference,
minimum, interval or centring, its guard computes both versions, enters the larger $p$ in Holm and requires both
verdicts to agree; looser and mixed changes lie outside it (Section~\ref{sec:deviations}).

``Uninformative'', undefined at the registry freeze, was defined after H3's result and before any hold-out label: a
90\% creator interval containing zero and either plus or minus the smallest effect of interest, or a verdict not
determinable, including ``not run for want of an instrument''. Support needs the Holm step, the registered support rule
and effect-size condition, the guard and A5 (a 90\% creator interval excluding zero). How a replication wave's $p$ values for H7 and H10 would join the family is not yet
specified.

\subsection{Verification without a person}
\label{sec:verification}

The owner declined a small person check that included a listening sample (A8) and asked for verification by models
instead (A11); every rule that named a person got a model substitute, with no floor lowered. Agreement with people,
construct validity and corrections for imperfect labels \cite{teblunthuis2023misclassification,egami2023imperfect}
need a person-coded sample and cannot be estimated.

A known-answer battery of texts built from development windows with planted answers tests behaviour
\cite{ribeiro2020beyond}; if such texts are easier than speech (not tested), recovery is an upper bound for the
corpus, not a measure of correctness on it. On the frozen voice instrument the small model recovers \KaKoneRSmallRevThree{} of planted
relayed passages (90\% \KaKoneRSmallRevThreeLo--\KaKoneRSmallRevThreeHi); the sentence-code and title arms sit at ceiling.

In the cross-vendor check an Anthropic model, of the family that took the lead's decisions and drafted this paper,
read \VtrUnits{} units of \VtrCreators{} seen check creators blind. Against the OpenAI majority its design-weighted
kappa on claimed, own and other voice is \VtrKappa{} (90\% \VtrKappaLo--\VtrKappaHi), a pass; unweighted,
\VtrKappaUnweighted; on the \VtrDisagreeUnits{} units where the small and middle models' first runs disagree,
\VtrDisagreeKappa{} (90\% \VtrDisagreeKappaLo--\VtrDisagreeKappaHi), below the floor. About \VtrHeavySharePct\% of the
weight lies on \VtrHeavyUnits{} units that both models call not claimed. The figures, computed just before the pass
rule was written and opened after the instrument freeze, cover voice only (Table~\ref{tab:crossvendor}). Evaluators
can favour their own outputs \cite{panickssery2024evaluators}; the registry records neither who drafted the prompts and
codebooks nor whether those agents saw these units.
