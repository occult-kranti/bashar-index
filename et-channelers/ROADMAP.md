### Phase 7a: shorts and short recordings against the main corpus (added 2026-11-14, at the owner's request; after collection)

A separate analysis, run once the transcripts of both kinds are in. **The main corpus is every recording over
5 minutes, with no upper limit** (nothing in the pipeline is limited to an hour: the queues keep every length,
and their `too_short` and `too_long` counts are 0). **Shorts, and any recording of 5 minutes or less, form a
group of their own**, compared to the main corpus with the method below. The channel listings already say which
is which: 16,115 shorts against 42,584 videos and live streams on the 63 channels listed.

* **Question 1, repetition.** Is a short new material, or a piece of a long video? For every short, look for
  its words in the same channel's long transcripts (overlapping five-word runs; a short is an *excerpt* when
  most of its runs sit together in one long video, a *rewording* when the words differ but the passage
  matches by meaning, *new* otherwise). Report per channel the share of each, which long videos the excerpts
  come from, and how long after the long video the short appeared. Do the same across channels, because
  clip channels cut other people's sessions (the Bashar list has 11 of them).
* **Question 2, topics.** Fit one vocabulary and one set of topics on the long videos only, then score the
  shorts with it, so that the two are measured with the same ruler. Compare topic shares (by words, not by
  count of videos), TF-IDF terms that set shorts apart from long videos (log-odds with a prior, so that rare
  words do not win by chance), and length, pace and question-and-answer share. Per channel and overall.
* **Question 3, what the shorts leave out.** Which topics of the long videos never reach a short, and which
  are over-represented there. For Bashar, set this beside the topic shares the Bashar Index already
  measured for its 75 recordings (`data/topic_share.csv`).
* **What it needs.** Transcripts for shorts and long videos of the same channels: YouTube's captions where
  they exist (the laptop lane), speech-to-text otherwise. Re-runs and re-uploads of one recording counted
  once (the twin and duplicate marks in the catalogue).
* **What it gives.** Tables and figures of numbers only, a section in the dashboard, and a methods note. No
  transcript text, and quotes within the 30-word limit.
* **Done when** every channel with at least 20 shorts and 20 long videos has its three answers, and a
  second reader has re-run the numbers from the saved transcripts.

### Phase 7c: years, recent recordings, predictions and warnings, timelines, question banks (added 2026-11-14, at the owner's request)

Built on the same corpus groups as Phase 7a (main corpus over 5 minutes; shorts and short recordings apart),
with texts from the source and speech-to-text kept apart in every table. The methods, statistics and sources
are in [`ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`](ANALYSIS_PLAN.md), written from a deep-research survey run with agents; its
section "Timelines, predictions and question banks (deep research, 2026-11-14)" is the current method for
everything below, and replaces the earlier per-channel caps.

* **By year, for each channel.** Recordings, hours, words, topics, vocabulary, share of audience questions,
  named beings, star systems and places, per year; year-over-year change with confidence intervals, and a
  correction for uneven sampling (few early recordings, many recent ones).
* **The last 5, 10, 50 and 100 recordings** of each channel against its whole history: what is new in topics
  and wording, length and pace, how often predictions and warnings come up. The last N is compared with the
  N just before it, against placebo windows from earlier in the channel, so a channel needs at least 2N
  recordings; one with fewer is reported as such, not padded.
* **Every prediction and warning.** A small model reads every transcript and marks each statement about the
  future: what is predicted or warned of, conditional or not, how sure, and the date or period it points to,
  resolved against the recording's own date. The same prediction repeated in several recordings or by several
  channels is linked, not counted twice. From this: **a timeline for each channel and one combined timeline**,
  each point linked to its recording and time stamp. The rules pass that finds the candidates and resolves
  their target windows was built on 2026-11-15 (`pipeline/prediction_timeline.py`), and a pilot of the model pass
  ran the same day (`pipeline/prediction_pilot.py`, HANDOFF section 5: three samples, about 56% of windowed
  candidates real predictions); the bulk model pass is next. Checking predictions against what happened comes later,
  only for specific and checkable ones, and with the scoring rules written down first.
* **Blackouts of the power grid and the internet** (added 2026-11-16, at the owner's request; ANALYSIS_PLAN
  12.4.1). Who has predicted or warned that the power grid will fail or be cut, or that the internet,
  communications or electronics will go down, with the date said, the time given and whether it has passed;
  **first in each channel's newest 10 videos** (through their titles, and through twins already here until the
  owner's run brings their captions and audio), then the newest 20 and 50, then everything stored. A reusable
  theme search (`pipeline/theme_search.py`, the theme as a data file), checked by two blinded coders and a recall
  probe; the titles that name the theme make a priority queue for the owner's computer. Counts public, text private.
* **Datasets the small model builds** (Claude Haiku in batches through the Message Batches API; agents run
  only the pilots and the adjudication; larger models re-label a sample blind, and agreement is reported): question-and-answer pairs from audience sessions, question banks grouped by theme,
  per-recording summaries, dated statements, predictions and warnings. Every row carries the item id and the
  time stamp or offset it came from. Rows that contain transcript text stay in the private store; counts,
  labels and ids can be published.
* **Dashboard.** Build the per-year tables, the last-N comparison and the two timelines first, from these

* **Done when** every channel with at least 20 recordings in the main corpus has its per-year table, its

## 5. Risks

| Risk | What it does | What the plan does about it |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube refuses downloads (seen on 2026-11-11 from a cloud server) | no audio | run from the owner's computer; the scripts stop after three blocks; no workaround is used; fall back to audio already held |
| Rights and terms | media is held by its rights holders; downloading is outside YouTube's terms | private storage, hashes only in public outputs, release only with the paper after a rights check |
| Speech-to-text errors on names, music, silence | wrong words, invented text in silent parts | voice-activity filter on; human-checked sample; pin the model; list files with no or repeated segments |
| Library changes | tools break between versions (already happened once: faster-whisper with PyAV 19) | versions in the requirements and the manifests; decoding done by ffmpeg; tests |
| Counts drift | the totals are from 2026-10-23 and are floors | `discover.py` records coverage; `inventory.py` regenerates the tables |
| Duplicates and re-uploads | the same session counted twice | fingerprint audio before analysis |
| Disk and time | 154 GB of audio and weeks of GPU time for the full set | sample first (Phase 5); measure before scaling |
| Data leaks into the repository | rights breach | `.gitignore` rules, Git-folder refusal in every script, `guard_no_raw_media.py`, review before each push |
