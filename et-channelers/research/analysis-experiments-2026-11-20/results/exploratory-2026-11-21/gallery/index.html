Exploratory figure gallery

Figures from the exploratory round of a registered study of online channelling recordings: 101 creators who present messages as channelled from non-human sources, of whom 71 were looked at before the registry was frozen (the seen creators) and 23 form the hold-out. The page has three parts: the second-round experiments, the exploratory outputs of the round's packages, and the items built for the paper review before the round. Each figure states its finding in its title; its caption says what is plotted, over which creators and with which interval.

Exploratory (registry A2); not a test of H1-H10. Hold-out outputs beside the seen ones, never pooled.

  • Exploratory; not a test of H1-H10. This is the exploratory round of a registered study (registry A2). The registered first-round tests ran and were published before the round began; nothing here tests the hypotheses H1 to H10 or changes a verdict. Every figure is a description to be checked later, never a confirmation.
  • Seen creators by default. Unless its title or caption says otherwise, a figure rests on the 71 seen creators, the creators looked at before the registry was frozen. A few date panels use only the publication dates and durations of all 101 creators, which the study does not count as contact with the hold-out; none of them splits by group.
  • Hold-out beside seen, never pooled. The hold-out outputs registered for after freeze 2 (marked “hold-out beside seen”, mostly in packages HOLD and HOLD2) show the hold-out creators beside the seen creators. They are exploratory replications: never pooled with the seen creators into one estimate, and never called a confirmation. The hold-out is the tail of an English-first run and is not exchangeable with the seen creators, so read each of them beside the balance figures (rv-D2 and rv-D2-H). Creators in neither group appear only as a group of their own.
  • The creator is the unit. Most figures show distributions over creators (histograms, quantile bands, box summaries, binned shares) or creator-balanced means with their intervals. Some draw an estimate pooled over another unit (entries, windows, statements, hits or recordings) or a count; their captions name that unit. No point, bar, row or label stands for a single creator or recording, and no figure carries names, channel names, creator codes, video titles or quotations.
  • Small cells pooled. Every plotted bin, cell or group rests on 5 or more creators. Smaller ones are pooled with a neighbour or into “other”, or dropped, and the caption says which. A share whose misses come from fewer than 5 creators reads “not published (misses from fewer than 5 creators)”, never a bound near 100%.
  • Model-coded labels, not checked by a person. Labels made by language models under frozen instruments unless an output says otherwise are model-coded and were not checked by a person. Where models, readers or methods are compared, a figure shows their agreement, never accuracy; recovery on constructed known-answer texts is an upper bound, not a measure of correctness on the corpus. Wording-pattern rates are cue counts until their precision is known.
  • Recognizer output. Pace, pause and coverage measures come from the recognizer's words and word times. They are recognizer-output measures, not direct measures of speaking.
  • Within a creator's span. Change over time is described inside each creator's own span of upload dates, never as a trend of the field. No prediction is said to have come true or to have failed.
  • Interval levels differ. Packages use 90% or 95% intervals, pointwise, simultaneous or family-wise; every caption states its level.
  • Contacts flagged. A caption that says “Made after a hold-out contact on” a date belongs to an output made or changed after an unplanned contact with hold-out material. The contacts are logged and listed in the paper's limitations; the outputs are still reported.
  • Corrections of 2026-11-26 (the third-round programme's r3-P35 under the lead's ruling 13; D72 and D73). Every caption that rests on labels (voice, asks, forecasts, sentence codes, titles, recording kinds and the outputs built on them) carries this validity status, whether or not it repeats it: model-coded; agreement only, never accuracy; not checked by a person; relayed voice alone below its floor; on the hold-out reliability sample, the forecast class of record and the sentence codes' stability below their floors; forecast checkability and title presentation kept as records after failed gates (the gate sample and the hold-out reliability sample drew harder titles than the rest of the seen roster (r3-P20); outside them, presentation agreement was higher). LAB-A's captions: the known-answer battery scores an own offer inside a claimed passage as own voice and holds no arm with an offer presented in the source's voice. A null is read with its detectable size (r3-P33; for E9 about 2.6 shared runs per pair on 6 creators); a null without one is not read as independence or as the absence of an effect. No measure in this study can tell whether a non-human source speaks; every measure describes the speech in these recordings and how the creators present it. E5-fig-1 was corrected (D72, D73, r3-P35). The release README lists every correction.
