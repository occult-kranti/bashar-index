# Further analysis experiments: catalogue of the analysis panel, 2026-11-20

**Status, 20 November 2026, evening.** The transcription run has finished (1,697 audio files). The registry of
hypotheses is [frozen](registry.md); H3 was tested and dropped by the owner; the bridge between the two
recognizer models (BR, part a) has run. Both results are in [`results/`](results/README.md). The numbers in
section 2 below still come from the snapshot of 1,213 transcripts and have not yet been recomputed on the
finished run. What runs next is in the [analysis handoff](../../knowledge-transfer/analysis-handoff-2026-11-20.md).

This catalogue adds 28 experiments to the analysis plan for the cohort of 101 creators (newest 20 recordings each). It was written by a panel of four roles: a senior mathematical data analyst (lead), a computational social scientist, a visual exploration specialist and a challenger who reviewed every proposal. Two literature studies (ICWSM; related venues), an audit of the plan against the data, and a reading of 42 transcripts came first.

How to read it:

- 40 experiments were proposed. 22 survive, with 17 others merged into them and 1 dropped. 6 more were added because the challenger found them missing. Section 6 lists every merge and rejection.
- Each experiment has a two-letter id. Value is high, medium or low. Cost is one of: hours, a day, days, labelling (model labels plus human checks), new data (GPU or new collection).
- Every number marked pilot, first pass or crude comes from an unfinished transcription run and from code that was not reviewed. Treat it as an order of magnitude.
- No transcript text appears here. Creators are not named.

## 1. What was studied

**ICWSM.** 32 papers from the International AAAI Conference on Web and Social Media (2017 to 2026) were read in full text by an automated extractor, 9 more at abstract level, plus 10 neighbouring sources. Any number quoted from them must be re-checked against the PDF. Methods that transfer to this cohort: the account as the unit of inference, with account-level resampling or intercepts and a check that leaves out the largest account [7][15][17]; coverage and skew tables [2][22]; speech-to-text error measured by stratum or by hand [19][26]; machine labels checked per task with a tuning set, a held-out set and a consistency score, and estimates corrected for misclassification [18][27]; narrative clusters with a similarity threshold validated by hand and per language [10][11][12][13]; keyness counted once per account [15][30]; event-time plots and date-shuffle nulls [4][5][6][8][13][14]; and coding practice from religion and conspiracy studies [1][21][24][25]. The existing plan is stricter on inference than most of the papers read, so its corrections, cluster bootstrap and equivalence tests stay; the venue adds presentation and validation routine. Quasi-experimental designs need many observations on both sides of a date and do not fit a newest-20 cohort. Four searches found no ICWSM paper on channelling, prophecy or failed predictions.

**Related work.** A second study covered neighbouring venues (CSCW, The Web Conference, ACL and EMNLP, Interspeech and ICASSP, methods journals, sociology of religion): 98 references, 11 opened, 79 confirmed by search at abstract level; the 3 unverified ones are not cited here. It supports: bounding recognition error with a human-corrected subsample and injected errors [32][37]; flagging text the recognizer invents on non-speech audio [33][34]; a closed name list, because entity recognizers fail on speech [35]; word time stamps for speech rate and for pauses of 0.2 seconds or longer, nothing finer [36]; no word-list emotion scores [39]; translated text only where stated, because machine translation preserved term matrices in formal writing [41] but flattens lexical richness [43], with multilingual sentence embeddings as the alternative [42]; a forecast ledger with verifiability classes [47], proper scores only where certainty is graded [45] and Dawson's categories for responses to failure [44]; topic shares treated as compositions [62][63][64][65]; small-sample methods when a group has few creators [68][69][70][71]; a variance partition for every measure [67]; power by simulation [74]; labelled analytic choices [73]; one validated list per construct [75]. Two published claims can be checked here: slower delivery in trance [51] and a similarity comparison across 3 channelers made without controls [50]. Not found: a computational study of failed predictions with follow-up, a comparison of trance speech with the same person's ordinary speech, and any validation of the recognizer's speech-to-English output.

### 2.3 What the reading of 42 transcripts added

42 finished transcripts from 39 creators were read (27 in full), chosen by stratum, not at random, with three crude passes over 1,201 transcripts.

- A recording filed under a channeler is often not channelled speech. Of 34 sampled recordings with 300 or more words: 17 hold a channelled voice or relayed message, 6 are interviews or podcasts without channelling, 5 guided meditations, 4 own-voice talks, 1 a hypnosis session, 1 an event advert.
- Extraterrestrial vocabulary is a minority. Share of 1,129 English transcripts containing the term family: soul 61%, frequency or vibration 61%, breath 42%, ascension 30%; any extraterrestrial or alien word 19%.
- Six speaking modes were seen: an embodied being in the first person, a relay in the creator's own voice, a scripted persona read without pauses, a client in trance, spoken non-language, and a meditation guide.
- The recognizer inserts a two-word thanks phrase into running speech: 1,641 segments in 55% of English transcripts, 80% of them starting 27 to 30 seconds into a 30-second decoding window. It invents short segments in music and silence (270 isolated segments in 9% of recordings; 39% start exactly on a 30-second multiple).
- Names of beings are the least reliable words: 3 to 7 spellings of one name inside one recording.
- Question marks do not measure audience questions. Only 2 of 42 sampled recordings hold real audience question-and-answer.
- The stored non-speech figure is zero for 138 of 1,201 transcripts and must be recomputed as audio seconds minus speech seconds.
- Translated views loop (up to 70 repeats), invent filler and replace names; word counts in Japanese are not words.

### 2.4 The earlier single-channeler results

The earlier Bashar Index study analysed 75 model-written digests of one channeler. Its headline numbers: the top three topics hold 31.1% of content; one of four registered tests was confirmed (topic mix differs by content type, Cramér's V 0.212, interval 0.211 to 0.300); a ledger of 164 dated claims showed 73 window changes with a median slip of 1.0 year per year. Its known defects are recorded in the plan: content type is confounded with batch, 7 of the 10 commonest terms are the prompt's own examples, word counts are model guesses. This cohort holds 17 short clips of that channeler (0.56 hours), so the two cannot be compared. The old tables are used here only to test code.

### 2.5 What these numbers suggest

1. The unit of inference is the creator, about 100 of them. Recordings are not independent.
2. Recognizer confidence follows delivery style between creators and something else inside a recording. It is a covariate, not a quality score, until it is calibrated against hand transcripts.
3. A recording is a mixture of parts (frame, main part, meditation, questions, selling, close). Content statistics need a part-of-recording label first.
4. Newest 20 is a quota. A creator's window covers anything from 3 days to 15 years. Order is not time and nothing here describes a history.
5. Language means five creators, a different recognizer model and a translation step at once. Only description is possible.
6. Artefacts are frequent enough to move counts of thanks, closings, questions and names. They must be removed first and every result shown with and without the filter.
7. 23 creators with 366 recordings were almost untranscribed when every pilot ran (18 of them, with 286 recordings, are not flagged non-English). They are the only part of this cohort that no pilot has seen.

## 3. Rules every experiment carries

These merge the challenger's safeguards with the notes of the three proposers. Where they conflicted, the challenger's version stands.

- **R1. The creator is the unit.** Resample creators, then recordings within a creator. Cohort values weight creators equally; word-pooled versions go in tables only. Every figure prints creators, recordings and words, and effective n where it applies. Every pooled number has a leave-one-creator-out range. No recording-level or window-level p-values.
- **R2. Seen means exploratory.** Pilots, crude counts and the reading sample used all 71 creators with 5 or more transcripts, so an odd-against-even split of those creators is not a hold-out. Before any pending transcript is analysed, freeze one registry: at most 10 confirmatory hypotheses with their estimands, procedures, lexicons, name lists, thresholds, event lists and a code hash, with Holm correction across the registry. Confirm on the 23 hold-out creators and on the replication wave (RW). Everywhere else report estimates and intervals without significance marks. Tuning a threshold on half the creators still limits over-fitting, but the other half does not become a confirmation sample.
- **R3. Every statistic has a stated reference distribution** and p = (b + 1) / (m + 1). The number of permutations is sized so that the smallest attainable p can pass the correction in use. Each null is shown to be able to fail: plant a known effect in the real design and recover it.
- **R4. Intervals.** One interval procedure per estimand is fixed beforehand; the coverage it reached in the coverage audit (CA) is printed with it. Curves get simultaneous bands. Per-creator values are shown raw beside shrunken. A creator is described as extreme only if its rank interval says so. One creator against about 90 gets a percentile, not a test: the smallest attainable p is 1/91 = 0.011.
- **R5. Clean first.** Remove the recognizer's inserted thanks phrase (by position rule, not by words alone), isolated short segments on the 30-second grid, repeated segments, loops and stock credits. Recompute non-speech as audio minus speech seconds. Count duplicate audio once. Report with and without the filter.
- **R6. One text kind and one recognizer model per comparison.** Text kinds are never pooled.
- **R7. No word list and no model label is used until two people have measured its precision and recall** on one shared, creator-stratified probability sample (HR). Gates: kappa at least 0.6; list precision at least 0.8. Report precision and recall separately, consistency over 5 model runs, and error by creator group, length band and language, with the misclassification-corrected estimate beside the raw one. Delivery labels come from listening. Two models agreeing is not a human check. Hand-work is budgeted once, across experiments.
- **R8. Robustness.** Each headline is rerun on the better-transcribed half, within length band, within delivery format and without the largest creator. Report the bootstrap interval of the change against a margin stated beforehand, not whether two intervals overlap.
- **R9. Estimators.** Rates use words or speech minutes as the denominator (per 1,000 words here). An estimator is accepted only if its absolute Spearman correlation with log tokens is under 0.15. Parts of a whole are analysed as log-ratios; raw shares are never correlated.
- **R10. Newest 20 is a quota.** No trend, origin, first or history claims. Order is not time. Upload date is not the date of speaking. Timing analyses use exact-day dates (or intervals of 7 days or less where stated), compare creators only inside shared calendar spans, and fix dates, events and word lists before any text is read.
- **R11. Language means five creators.** Case description only: marked points in their own units, never pooled, no tests. Figures carry the label "recognizer model not bridged" until BR is done.
- **R12. Topics, clusters and axes.** At least 5 seeds; a stability gate fixed beforehand; "no stable structure" is a result. Groups are never tested on the features that formed them. A visual pattern is reported only after a replicate on a split, or a lineup judged by 5 viewers who have not seen the data [93].
- **R13. No outside comparison corpus exists.** Each experiment names its comparison inside the corpus. Nothing is called distinctive of channelling until the comparison set (CS) exists.
- **R14. Figures.** Each shows an interval or band and its n, states its transform, and has a CSV behind it. Dots on a common scale, not bars, for rates [102][103]. Heat maps fade cells whose interval crosses the reference [97], use a diverging palette only for signed values, and carry side strips for words and median recognizer confidence. Solid marks need 8 or more recordings, hollow marks 5 to 7, fewer go in tables only; this rule is kept only if VP's ordered split supports it. Each figure prints its snapshot date and counts.
- **R15. Outputs and conduct.** Public outputs hold numbers, ids and derived labels: no transcript text, no audience members, no health details. Private: single-recording timelines, example lines, shared passages, hand transcripts and any inference about a person. No named ranking on selling, forecasts or certainty; creators are named publicly only for length, pace and non-speech share. Claims are reported as claims with no truth label. The project keeps a datasheet and a removal route.
- **R16. Housekeeping.** Re-check cited numbers against the PDFs. No heavy computation until the transcription worker has ended. Rerun every pilot number at close.

## 4. The experiments

Six groups. Inside a group the order is value first, then cost. Section 7 gives the order of work, which follows dependencies, not this ranking. "Hold-out creators" are the 23 creators of section 2.5, item 7. "Seen creators" are the 71 with 5 or more transcripts at the snapshot. Rule numbers (R1 to R16) refer to section 3.

### 4.1 Measurement and inference base

#### T1. Coverage and recognizer ledger [value high; cost hours]

- **Question.** Which creators can carry which analysis, where are the gaps in transcription, text kind, dates and recognizer model, and do missing recordings differ from present ones?
- **Data and unit.** All 101 creators and 1,976 selected recordings, joined to the run ledger, each transcript's provenance record and the published recording-date table. One row per creator; marks inside a row are single recordings. Every creator appears, including those without a transcript. For hold-out creators only counts are computed before the registry is frozen.
- **Method.** Counts and quantiles, no test. Non-speech recomputed (R5). Median recognizer confidence with interquartile range. Missingness: recordings held (23), without audio (30) and held in other text kinds (214) compared with transcribed ones by creator, length band and date period, as row percentages and standardised differences with creator-bootstrap intervals. Weekdays in one fixed time zone (UTC). Weekday concentration per creator is set against a simulated range under equal weekday probability at that creator's n. Sort order and flag thresholds fixed before rendering.
- **Outputs.**
  - Table T1-1, the ledger. Columns: creator id; language; recognizer model; text kinds; counts selected / audio archived / transcribed in this run / other text kind / held / audio missing; exact against estimated dates; audio hours; speech hours; words; window of the newest 20 in days as a number plus a bar on one shared log axis (3 to 5,629 days); a strip of 20 squares in listing order, fill = status; a sparkline of 20 bars, height = log10 minutes, fill = non-speech share in 4 steps; a dot with interquartile bar for median recognizer confidence on a shared axis (-0.30 to -0.05); count of flagged recordings (under 50 words, half or more non-speech, loops, non-language). Footer rows give cohort quartiles.
  - Fig T1-2, calendar heat map: rows = creators ordered by median gap between uploads; columns = the last 52 ISO weeks; cell = uploads among the newest 20 (0, 1, 2, 3 or more); estimated dates as a pale bar across their interval; the area before a creator's window hatched; top margin = creators active per week.
  - Fig T1-3, weekday heat map: rows = creators ordered by weekday concentration; 7 columns; cell = share of that creator's exact-day uploads; margin = n.
  - Table T1-4, missingness: status by creator group, length band and date period, with intervals.
  - Table T1-5: creator, window start and end, days spanned, median gap, top weekday and share, exact and estimated dates.
- **Follows.** Dataset and skew tables [22]; with and without transcript by group [2]; the podcast corpus's reporting [26]; rows as layers over time [96]. Extends the coverage heat map of the 2026-11-16 panel.
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** It is a snapshot until the run closes: date and counts are printed and it is rerun at close. Duplicate audio is counted once. The confidence column follows delivery style (Spearman 0.49 with pace), so it is named "recognizer confidence" and is not a ranking of accuracy. 397 dates are intervals and 36 ids lack a date row: both are shown, not dropped. The upload date is not the date of speaking (R10).

#### VP. Variance partition and reliability atlas [value high; cost hours]

Absorbs the stability curve (visual F2) and the between-and-within correlation matrix (MC).

- **Question.** For each measure, how much variance lies between creators, between recordings of one creator and inside a recording, and how many recordings does a creator-level number need before it can be shown or ranked?
- **Data and unit.** English-original transcripts from one recognizer model after the artefact filter. Three nested levels: window (100 consecutive words for timing measures, 200 for word rates; a second run with 400), recording, creator. Creators with at least 2 recordings that hold at least 2 windows each; at most 40 evenly spaced windows per recording. Twenty measures fixed in advance: log articulation rate (gaps of 2 seconds or more removed), pause-time share, long pauses per 100 words, segment log-probability, mean word probability, rates per 1,000 words of contractions, fillers, second-person pronouns, first-person singular pronouns, first-person plural pronouns, the future auxiliary, hedges, numbers, question marks, sales terms and calendar terms, types per window, repeated five-word share; at recording level only: non-speech share and log length. Each word-list measure is marked "unvalidated" until HR has measured its precision.
- **Method.** Nested method-of-moments analysis of variance for all 20 measures. Negative-binomial mixed models with a log-words offset only for the 4 or 5 zero-heavy rates, shares on the latent scale [67]. Interval: nested bootstrap (creators, then recordings, windows kept), 2,000 resamples. Derived per measure: design effect 1 + (mean recordings per creator - 1) x ICC; effective n; reliability of a creator mean from k recordings, R_k = k x rho / (1 + (k - 1) x rho), for k = 2, 5, 10, 20; smallest difference detectable at 80% power between creator groups of 10 against 80 and 30 against 60, by simulation [74]. Stability check: split-half rank correlation of creator medians for k = 2 to 10, with halves split by order (odd against even listing rank, and older against newer), set beside R_k. Between and within: for the 8 to 10 measures that reach reliability 0.7 at the recordings available, between-creator and within-creator correlation matrices from a multivariate one-way random-effects decomposition, pronouns entered as log-ratios, intervals by creator bootstrap; list every pair whose sign reverses between levels [81]. Components and creator maps only if parallel analysis passes [90], labelled exploratory, with no named axes. Estimation only: no tests. Sensitivity: no window cap, no artefact filter, recordings of 10 minutes or more only.
- **Outputs.**
  - Fig VP-1: horizontal stacked bars, one row per measure, three segments (between creators, between recordings, within recording), rows sorted by creator share, whisker = 95% interval of the creator share; 100-word and 400-word versions side by side.
  - Fig VP-2: reliability curves, x = recordings per creator (1 to 20), y = reliability, one labelled line per measure, reference line at 0.80, ordered split-half points overlaid for k = 2 to 10, creators available printed under each k.
  - Fig VP-3: heat map, measures by level (3 columns), variance share printed in each cell.
  - Fig VP-4: one heat map of the reliable measures, lower triangle = between-creator correlation, upper triangle = within-creator correlation, cells whose interval includes 0 left blank, sign-reversal pairs outlined.
  - Table VP-1: measure, validation status, creators, recordings, windows, three shares with intervals, design effect, effective n, R_2, R_5, R_10, R_20, smallest k reaching 0.8 under the ordered split, smallest detectable difference in natural units.
  - Table VP-2: sign-reversal pairs with both correlations and intervals.
- **Pilot, exploratory.** See section 2.2. With random halves the split-half correlation at 5 recordings was 0.66 to 0.83 across nine measures and 0.76 to 0.91 at 8; random halves ignore serial dependence, so these are optimistic.
- **Follows.** Variance shares from mixed models [67]; power by simulation [74]; nested count models [7]; within against between effects [81]; parts of a whole not correlated raw [64]; sensitivity to a minimum-count threshold [21].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** The within share belongs to the window length used: both lengths are shown. Long recordings dominate without the cap. The creator share absorbs format and guests: rerun within delivery state once SG passes its gate. Every caption says whether it shows the recording-level ICC (pace 0.58) or the window-level share (0.32); the two are not comparable. With about 70 creators and 20 measures a covariance matrix is noisy, so the matrix is limited to reliable measures. The roster is purposive: the shares describe this cohort only.

#### EB. Shrunken creator values, funnel plots and rank intervals [value high; cost hours]

Absorbs the ranked dot plot with funnel (visual F3).

- **Question.** Which per-creator values and which most-or-least rankings can be told apart from noise once each creator's small number of recordings is taken into account?
- **Data and unit.** Creator-level delivery measures: length, pace, non-speech share, recognizer confidence, pause rates, transcript vocabulary at 1,000 tokens, repeated five-word share. Topic shares, term rates and any labelled measure enter only after the labels pass their gates. Unit = creator; precision = its effective recordings (from SD) and words. Creators with 1 to 3 transcripts are listed with raw values only.
- **Method.** Gaussian measures: empirical-Bayes estimate theta_c = mu + B_c x (mean_c - mu), B_c = tau2 / (tau2 + sigma2 / k_c), tau2 and sigma2 from VP, k_c the effective number of recordings; interval from the posterior variance plus the uncertainty of mu and tau2 by parametric bootstrap (2,000). Rates: gamma-Poisson with word offsets. Shares: beta-binomial, or a logit scale for heavy tails. Ranks: 4,000 draws of creator values, each ranked; report median rank, 95% rank interval, probability of top 5 and of bottom 5. Funnel: raw creator value against precision with limits mu +/- 1.96 and +/- 3.09 x sqrt(tau2 + sigma2 / k), tau2 from VP; naive limits (tau2 = 0) drawn dashed for contrast. Reference: 999 simulated cohorts with tau2 = 0 and the real k_c. Shrinkage is toward the cohort mean until format labels pass SG's gate, then within format class. Reporting rule: a creator is described as extreme only if its 95% rank interval lies inside the top or bottom fifth. Percentiles, never per-creator tests (R4).
- **Outputs.**
  - Fig EB-1, one per measure: caterpillar plot, raw mean as open dot, shrunken mean as filled dot, joined by a line, 95% interval, creators sorted by shrunken value, grey band = cohort interquartile range, right margin = recordings and words.
  - Fig EB-2: funnel panels, x = recordings or log words, y = raw creator value, inner 95% and outer 99.8% limits, naive limits dashed, only points outside the outer limit labelled.
  - Fig EB-3: heat map, creators by measures, cell = shrunken z-score, hatched where the rank interval spans more than half the cohort; coded ids except for length, pace and non-speech share.
  - Table EB-1: creator, measure, recordings, effective recordings, raw value, shrunken value, interval, percentile, median rank, rank interval, P(top 5), P(bottom 5), inside or outside the funnel limits.
- **Pilot, exploratory.** With plain binomial limits on the question-ending share, 56 of 71 creators fall outside the 95% limits and 43 outside 99.8% (over-dispersion factor 35): naive limits are unusable. From the audit's ICCs, a creator mean of pace has reliability 0.87 from 5 recordings and 0.73 from 2; question share 0.67 and 0.45.
- **Follows.** Shrinkage estimators [78]; limits of league tables [79]; funnel plots with over-dispersion [80]. The plan has a funnel only for prediction counts (12.4).
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Shrinking toward one mean assumes creators are exchangeable, but formats differ (five creators hold 44% of non-speech time): hence within-format shrinkage after the gate and delivery measures only before it. Neighbouring recordings are dependent, so k overstates information: effective k from SD is used. Pooled rates are dominated by a creator's longest recordings (one 540-minute file holds 6 minutes of speech): the caterpillar shows the mean of recordings, the funnel the pooled rate, and each caption says which. Naming has reputational weight: the rank-interval rule is a gate, and rankings on sales, forecasts and questions stay private (R15).

#### CA. Coverage audit, placebo contrasts, negative controls and power [value high; cost a day]

- **Question.** Do the interval and test procedures reach 95% coverage on data shaped like this cohort, and what is the smallest difference each registered contrast can detect, in the full cohort and in the hold-out?
- **Data and unit.** Synthetic cohorts generated from the fitted VP components on the real design (101 creators, the real recordings per creator and words per recording), and plasmode cohorts: real creators resampled with replacement with a known shift planted in a random subgroup. Unit: one simulated study; 1,000 per scenario, inner bootstrap 199. Negative controls use the real data.
- **Method.** Laid out as aims, data-generating mechanisms, estimands, methods and performance measures [76]. Two estimands: a difference between two creator groups and a creator-level Spearman correlation. Scenarios: creator ICC 0.2, 0.4, 0.6; group sizes 10 against 80 and 30 against 60; plus the hold-out design of 18 creators. One procedure per estimand is fixed beforehand from the few-cluster literature: for a group difference, a restricted-likelihood mixed model with a creator intercept and a small-sample t interval [71], with the wild cluster bootstrap [68][69] and the bias-reduced cluster-robust estimator [70] shown beside it; for a correlation, the creator percentile bootstrap. The simulation verifies the fixed procedure and prints its coverage; it does not choose among procedures. A naive recording-level interval is included as the warning case. Performance: coverage of the nominal 95% interval (Monte-Carlo standard error about 0.007 at 1,000 runs), mean width, type I error, power curve, smallest difference detectable at 80% power. Negative controls [77]: (i) 1,000 random splits of creators into two pseudo-groups give the empirical false-positive rate of each test family after its Holm step; (ii) two placebo contrasts: creators split by odd or even hash of the creator id, recordings split by odd or even hash of the video id; (iii) negative-control outcomes that content groups should not move: placebo word lists matched in frequency to each lexicon, the position of segment starts on the 30-second grid, and the parity of a recording's duration in seconds.
- **Outputs.**
  - Fig CA-1: heat map, rows = procedures, columns = scenarios, coverage in percent printed in each cell, cells more than two Monte-Carlo standard errors from 95 outlined.

#### QD. Quality dependence and artefact anatomy [value high; cost a day for stage 1, hours for stage 2 after HR]

Absorbs the quality plate (visual F13).

- **Question.** Where and when does the recognizer invent text, which measures move with transcript quality and at which level, and does recognizer confidence predict word error well enough to act as a gate?
- **Data and unit.** All English-original recordings, one recognizer model at a time; windows of 100 words; segment fields (mean log-probability, compression ratio, no-speech probability, word probability); artefact flags by rule. Stage 2 uses the HR excerpts in 60-second windows. Units: window, recording, creator.
- **Method, stage 1.** (1) Artefact anatomy: flagged segments by rule (inserted thanks phrase; isolated segments of 3 words or fewer; segments starting on a 30-second multiple; repeated segments; compression ratio over 2.4), by position in the 30-second decoding window and in the recording. (2) Quality index Q per window: first principal component of z-scores of mean log-probability, share of words with probability under 0.5, compression ratio and artefact count; loadings fixed on a development half of creators; built separately for each recognizer model. (3) Within-between model for each VP measure: the measure on the creator mean of Q, the recording mean minus the creator mean, and the window value minus the recording mean, with random intercepts for creator and recording [81]; three standardised slopes with nested-bootstrap intervals; Benjamini-Hochberg over the 60 slopes as a screen only. (4) Dose-response: each headline estimate recomputed after dropping the worst q% of windows by creator-relative Q, q = 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 50. A result is quality-robust if the paired-bootstrap interval of the change from q = 0 to q = 30 lies inside a margin stated beforehand (R8). (5) Forward error curve: substitutions, deletions and insertions injected at added rates of 5, 10 and 20%, substitutes drawn from the recognizer's own low-probability words; each headline estimate refitted 50 times per rate; a names-only variant. No back-extrapolated "corrected" estimate is reported.
- **Method, stage 2.** (6) Calibration: hand word error rate per window against mean word probability and against Q; isotonic fit; consistency bars from resampling under perfect calibration [95]; intervals by bootstrap over recordings; one line per stratum (talk, music-backed, meditation, second speaker). The fitted curve gives a corpus-relative gate that replaces the stock cut-off. (7) Error by stratum as intervals, no test, with name-error rate beside it.
- **Outputs.**
  - Fig QD-1: x = segment start modulo 30 seconds (0.5-second bins), y = segment log-probability; hexagon layer of segments of 3 words or fewer with inserted-thanks segments overplotted; histogram on x.
  - Fig QD-2: artefacts per 1,000 segments by position bin (20 bins), one line per rule, creator-bootstrap bands.
  - Fig QD-3: one dot per creator, x = median pace, y = median recognizer confidence, size = recordings, correlation with interval printed.
  - Fig QD-4: heat map, rows = 20 measures, columns = 3 levels, cell = standardised slope on Q printed, cells failing the screen in grey.
  - Fig QD-5: dose-response small multiples, one per headline estimate, x = percent of worst windows dropped, y = estimate with interval, margin shaded.
  - Fig QD-6: forward error curve, x = added error rate, y = estimate, one line per headline estimate, names-only variant dashed.
  - Fig QD-7 (stage 2): reliability diagram, x = mean word probability in 8 quantile bins, y = observed word accuracy, points with consistency bars, diagonal, histogram of windows under the axis, one line per stratum.

- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Log-probability is not error: it is calibrated in stage 2 and used as a covariate before that. Q learned on one model cannot be carried to the other. Dropping low-Q windows removes meditation and music passages selectively: Table QD-3 says which states are lost. Invented text in silence can have high confidence and escapes Q: the artefact rules use isolation and the 30-second grid. Creators do say thanks: the rule uses position, not the words alone.

#### SD. Serial dependence across the newest 20 [value medium; cost hours]

The drift study proposed as OR, reduced to what it can support.

- **Question.** Are neighbouring recordings of one creator more alike than distant ones, and how many effective recordings does a creator therefore have?
- **Data and unit.** Creators with at least 10 English-original recordings holding 1 minute of speech or more (68 at the pilot). Order = listing rank (rank 1 is the newest; confirmed on 798 of 798 adjacent dated pairs). (2) Lag-1 autocorrelation of within-creator residuals with a within-creator permutation reference (9,999). (3) Effective recordings per creator from the autocorrelation, passed to EB, CA and the ordered splits of VP and SM. (4) Whether a missing position depends on rank: odds ratio from a logistic model with a creator intercept. No test of drift.

- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Order is not time (R10): nothing here describes a creator's history. 214 other-text recordings and 30 without audio leave holes: rank is used, not a consecutive index, and step 4 checks the holes. ### 4.2 Inside a recording: structure and delivery

#### PO. Position curves over a recording's running time [value high; cost hours]

Absorbs the position heat map (visual F7), the edge-against-middle dumbbell (visual F8, stage 1) and the position arm of the ask ladder.

- **Question.** What do openings, middles and closings look like in pace, silence, address and selling, and are those shapes larger than chance alignment would give?
- **Data and unit.** English-original recordings with 300 or more words, faceted by length band (5 minutes or less, 5 to 20, 20 to 60, over 60); the headline curves use recordings of 10 minutes or more with at least 800 words (861 recordings, 83 creators at the pilot). Two time axes: 20 equal bins of running time, and the first and last 180 seconds in 15-second bins. About 18 rows in four groups: structure (speech coverage, pauses of 2 seconds, pauses of 8 seconds, pace ratio); recognizer (confidence minus the recording mean, inserted-thanks flags, isolated short segments); address and register (first-person singular, first-person plural, second-person pronouns, contractions, fillers); content markers (question-ending share, relay clauses, the future auxiliary, calendar terms, breath and body terms, sales terms, name-list hits). A bin needs at least 50 words. Unit: creator (its curve is the mean over its recordings).
- **Method.** Each recording is expressed relative to its own mean. Creator-balanced mean curve. 95% simultaneous sup-t band from 2,000 creator-bootstrap resamples [82]. One null: circular shift of each recording's bin sequence by a random offset (999 draws), which keeps autocorrelation and destroys alignment with position. Two named contrasts: U = mean of the first and last tenth minus the mean of the middle six tenths; T = last tenth minus first tenth. On the seen creators: estimates and bands only. On the hold-out creators: at most 4 registered contrasts tested against the shift null, Holm inside the registry (R2). Per-creator view: median within-recording difference (middle minus edge) with a bootstrap interval over recordings; creators are ordered by the shift in odd-numbered recordings and plotted from even-numbered ones, which guards against regression to the mean. Controls: without publisher-feed audio (92 recordings carry third-party adverts), with and without the artefact filter. No sales or calendar row is shown before its list precision is measured (100 hits per list, two readers). Ask-density curves by class are added once AK's labels pass. No typology of curves unless SM's stability gate is met.
- **Outputs.**
  - Fig PO-1: heat map, rows = features in four groups, columns = 20 position bins, colour = log2 ratio of the bin to the row mean (diverging, centred on 0, clipped at +/- 2), cells whose simultaneous interval includes 0 at reduced saturation, right margin = row mean in natural units and creators contributing; one facet per length band.
  - Fig PO-2: 12 small panels of curves, x = relative position, y = ratio to (or difference from) the recording mean, simultaneous band, shift-null band in grey.
  - Fig PO-3: the same measures for the first and last 180 seconds in absolute time.
  - Fig PO-4: dumbbell plot in three facets (contractions, first-person singular, pace): y = creators in the odd-recording order, hollow dot = edge value, filled dot = middle value, grey band = interquartile range of edge values, right margin = recordings; captioned "edge against middle".
  - Fig PO-5: heat maps for second-person rate, sales terms, speech coverage and pause share: rows = creators in an order fixed beforehand, columns = 20 bins, cell = creator mean ratio.
  - Table PO-1: measure, contrast, estimate, interval, creators (seen creators, exploratory). Table PO-2: registered contrasts on the hold-out creators with estimate, interval, shift-null p, Holm p. Table PO-3: precision of each word list.
- **Pilot, exploratory (83 creators, 10 bins).** Second-person pronouns rise from 37.9 to 57.5 per 1,000 words from the first to the last tenth. First-person singular is 34.3 in the first tenth against 25 to 27 in tenths 3 to 8. Sales terms are 1.45 in the first tenth, 0.57 to 0.83 in tenths 3 to 8, 2.13 in the last. Pace ratio is 1.02, about 0.985, then 1.04. Log-probability is lower by 0.009 and 0.012 in the last two tenths. - **Follows.** Position-normalised arcs [59][60]; simultaneous bands [82]; rows as layers [96]; fading uncertain cells [97]; position formats in [20][23].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** These contrasts were already seen on every eligible creator, so p-values on them would confirm nothing: hence the hold-out. Closing bins hold the inserted thanks, stock credits, the lowest speech coverage (0.73) and the lowest confidence: filter first, show both versions, print words per bin. Teasers copy later text into the opening (12.7% of recordings repeat their first 350 words later). Relative bins mix a 10-minute and a 2-hour recording: read the absolute-time panel beside them. The edge zone holds teasers, adverts, sales blocks and outro music, so it is never captioned as the creator's own voice. Shapes differ by format: facet by delivery state once SG passes.

#### LX. Length-free lexical diversity, repetition and formulaicity [value high; cost a day]

- **Question.** Which creators are lexically narrow, repetitive or formulaic once recording length is taken out of the estimate, and where in a recording do the formulas sit?
- **Data and unit.** English-original recordings from one recognizer model and one text kind with at least 1,000 tokens after artefact and loop removal; creators with at least 5 such recordings; fixed blocks of 1,000 tokens; names of beings normalised to one spelling from the closed name list. About a quarter of recordings fall under 1,000 tokens and are excluded; the creators this removes are listed on the figure. Unit: creator.
- **Method.** One primary diversity estimator: the expected number of types in a random 1,000-token subsample (exact hypergeometric formula), mean over blocks, reported as "transcript vocabulary at 1,000 tokens". Two others in a sensitivity table only: MTLD at threshold 0.72, forward and backward [61]; Hill numbers of order 0, 1, 2 at equal sample coverage 0.80 [91]. Repetition: the plan's five-word self-reuse (5.13) computed inside 1,000-token blocks; and the compression ratio of a block divided by the ratio for the same block with word order shuffled. Formulaicity: leave-one-recording-out coverage, where the inventory is every run of 4 to 8 words found in at least 3 of the creator's other recordings and coverage is the share of the held-out recording's tokens inside such runs; reference = the same with the inventory of a random other creator matched on recordings and tokens (200 draws); excess = own minus reference median. Templates: the first and last 150 words clustered within creator by normalised edit distance of 0.2 or less; share of recordings in the largest template. Acceptance rule (R9). Bound: types per 1,000 tokens in the hand transcript against the recognizer transcript of the same HR excerpts. Intervals by bootstrap over recordings for a creator and over creators for the cohort; shrinkage as in EB. No tests.
- **Outputs.**
  - Fig LX-1: length-bias panel, 6 scatter plots against log tokens with smoother and Spearman printed (plain type-token ratio, moving-average type-token ratio, types at 1,000, MTLD, repeated five-word share at full length, the same at 1,000).
  - Fig LX-2: dot plot with intervals, creators sorted by transcript vocabulary; second column = excess formula coverage; facets by delivery format; excluded creators listed beneath.
  - Fig LX-3: heat map, creators by position (opening, body, closing), cell = formula coverage printed; side column = share of recordings in the top opening template.
  - Fig LX-4: scatter, x = transcript vocabulary, y = excess formulaicity, dot size = tokens, loop-flagged creators marked.
  - Table LX-1: estimator, Spearman with length, creator ICC, reliability at 10 recordings, accepted yes or no. Table LX-2: creator ranks under the three diversity estimators. Table LX-3: hand against recognizer types per 1,000 tokens.

Several spellings of one name inflate types: names are normalised. The recognizer undercounts rare words: the measure is named transcript vocabulary and bounded by Table LX-3. Adverts and platform boilerplate count as formulas: runs shared by 10 or more creators are separated from a creator's own. Meditation scripts repeat by design: facet by format. #### SG. Delivery-state segmentation and the within-recording shift [value high; cost days, plus the HR boundary marking]

Absorbs the single-recording timeline strip (visual F6), stage 2 of the dumbbell (visual F8), the voice test of the grounds-for-belief proposal, and the one surviving step of the dropped question experiment.

- **Question.** Where inside a recording does the delivery change (frame, main part, meditation, question exchange, close), and how large is the shift in wording across that change compared with differences between creators?
- **Data and unit.** English-original recordings of 5 minutes or more. Sliding windows of 100 words stepped by 50. Cutting features, timing only: log articulation rate, pause-time share, long-pause count, mean word probability. Test features, wording only, never used for cutting: rates per 1,000 words of contractions, fillers, first-person singular, first-person plural and second-person pronouns, hedges and boosters. Validation: sample (c) of HR (60 fresh recordings, two markers working from audio) plus 200 windows labelled with a state. Unit: creator for the contrast, recording for validation.
- **Method.** Cutting features are z-scored within recording. Change points by penalised least squares (PELT) [83]; the penalty is set on 100 held-out recordings so that block-shuffled versions (blocks of 5 windows, 200 shuffles each) give one or more change points in at most 5% of cases. Segments are grouped into delivery states by a Gaussian mixture on creator-centred segment means, the number of states chosen by BIC within 3 to 7 on a development half of creators, then frozen. States are named by delivery (for example slow with long pauses; fast conversational). A state is called a channelled or relayed voice only where the hand labels support that reading. Validation, in this order: agreement between the two markers; boundary precision, recall and F1 within 30 seconds; state confusion matrix; kappa against the hand labels. Gate: boundary F1 at least 0.70 and kappa at least 0.60. If the gate fails, every downstream contrast by voice or state (in EB, GB, FL, AK, SV, TP, EV) is not run or is labelled exploratory. Contrast: in recordings with a frame segment and a main segment of at least 300 words each, the paired difference main minus frame per wording feature; creator means pooled by random-effects meta-analysis, giving a pooled shift, its interval and a 95% prediction interval for a new creator; the ratio of the absolute pooled shift to the between-creator SD of frame values, with creator-bootstrap interval; reference = sign-flip permutation of creator mean differences (9,999). A second version uses hand boundaries only. The hedge and booster shift waits for HR's deletion rate of hedges by delivery state; if deletion differs between states by more than the shift, the result is reported as not interpretable. Question candidates: 300 sampled candidates labelled by two readers as audience member, host, facilitator or the speaker.
- **Private marking instrument.** One strip per recording: x = running time; tracks for speech and non-speech with quiet stretches of 30 seconds or more outlined, speech rate per 30 seconds as a two-band horizon strip around the recording's own median, pause ticks (2 seconds, 8 seconds), recognizer confidence as grey level, artefact flags, question-ending segments, a register strip per 60 seconds, formula marks, sales terms, date and calendar terms, the future auxiliary, and one row per name family. Markers see only the first four tracks. No transcript words on the strip.
- **Outputs.**
  - Fig SG-1 (private): timeline strips, and one panel per creator with 10 or more recordings holding one row per recording coloured by delivery state.
  - Fig SG-2: per creator, share of speech time in each delivery state with bootstrap intervals, creators sorted by the share of the slowest state; coded ids.
  - Fig SG-3: forest plots in 4 panels (contractions, first-person singular, fillers, second-person): one row per creator, paired shift with interval, pooled diamond and prediction interval; creators ordered on odd recordings, values from even ones.
  - Table SG-1: marker agreement; boundary precision, recall, F1; confusion matrix; kappa. Table SG-2: per feature, pooled shift, interval, ratio to between-creator SD, permutation p, creators contributing. Table SG-3: the 300 question candidates by asker role.
- **Reading-sample facts.** See section 2.3: half of the sampled recordings held no channelled voice, and 2 of 42 held real audience questions.
- **Follows.** Change-point detection [83]; the claim that trance channels deliver the same text more slowly [51]; topic control in voice comparisons [38]; parts of a recording in the sermon study [30]; style compared across posting identities [24]; strip forms [100][101]; time-stamped spans in video transcripts [20].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Cutting where rate, pauses and wording change and then testing the shift on the same features inflates it by construction: cutting uses timing, testing uses wording. Validation on the recordings that produced the mode list would be circular: the 60 are fresh. There are no speaker labels, so a state change may be a guest, a host or a questioner (SP). Relayed messages do not change delivery and will be missed: this is stated with every result. The paired contrast covers only creators who frame their recordings, a selected subset: the number contributing is printed. Music under a meditation lowers word probability and can fake a boundary. Frame and main part also differ in topic, so a shift is delivery plus content. A strip localises health disclosures and clients even without text: strips are private (R15).

#### RH. Rhythm of speech and silence from word time stamps [value medium; cost a day, plus the HR listening labels]

Absorbs the speech-rate ridgelines (visual F4) and the delivery plane (visual F5).

- **Question.** Do creators differ in the rhythm of speech and silence in ways that separate scripted reading, trance-like delivery, conversation and guided meditation, or is delivery one continuum?
- **Data and unit.** Word time stamps of English-original recordings with at least 300 words, one recognizer model (1,134 recordings, 90 creators, 4.23 million gaps between words at the pilot; 25,239 gaps of 2 seconds or more, 422 of 30 seconds or more). Four primary features per recording: fillers per 1,000 words; pauses of 1 second or more per 100 words; long pauses (2 seconds or more) per 100 words; coefficient of variation of rate across 100-word windows. (The proposal's fourth feature, the memory of successive run lengths, depended on a 0.5-second cut and moves to the appendix; the 1-second pause rate replaces it.) Descriptive features: pauses of 0.3 seconds or more per 100 words, median pause, share of time in long pauses, articulation rate, rate per audio minute. Labels: sample (c) of HR, marked by listening as scripted, trance-like, conversation or meditation. Unit: creator.
- **Method.** (0) Instrument check first: gap start modulo 30 seconds, and the gap-length histogram from 0.1 to 1.5 seconds; any artefact band is dropped. (1) Description: per-creator distribution of log gap duration; weights of a two-component log-normal mixture as description, no likelihood-ratio tests. (2) Rate ridges: kernel density of words per speech minute per audio minute holding at least 20 seconds of speech, common range 40 to 260, one fixed bandwidth, equal area, each recording equal weight within its creator. (3) Delivery plane: x = gaps of 2 seconds or more per speech minute, y = fillers per 1,000 words, square-root scales. Cluster check on creator-level values: Gaussian mixtures with 1 to 6 components and the BIC curve; lineup of the real pooled scatter among 19 simulated from one fitted bivariate distribution, judged by 5 viewers who have not seen the data [93]. (4) Typology: multinomial logistic regression of the listening label on the 4 primary features, leave-one-creator-out cross-validation, balanced accuracy with creator-bootstrap interval against a label-permutation reference (999); then applied to all recordings. Creator values shrunken as in EB. Appendix only: burstiness, memory and the Fano factor of pause onsets with a within-recording shuffle reference [92].

#### CX. Content axis from name families, term families and topics [value high; cost a day for stage A; a day for stage B once the topic model exists]

#### SM. Creator similarity: noise ceiling, agreement between representations and cluster stability [value high; cost a day]

Absorbs the three similarity matrices in one row order (visual F10).

- **Question.** Which creators resemble each other, is that structure reproducible from half the data, does it survive a change of representation, and do embeddings show anything that word counts do not?
- **Data and unit.** Creators with at least 10 English-original recordings of 300 words or more (66 at the pilot; the rest listed). Four representations, each rarefied to equal tokens per creator with the token count printed: A = the 150 most frequent words, z-scored; B = content-word TF-IDF with names masked and each creator's formulas and third-party adverts removed (the plan's creator-balanced centroids); C = rhythm features from RH; D = topic composition from CX (Aitchison distance). A fifth, E = the centroid of sentence-embedding vectors of 150-word windows, enters only one question. Unit: creator; creator pair for description.
- **Method.** (1) Noise ceiling: each creator's recordings are split by order into an older and a newer half; Spearman correlation between the off-diagonal cells of the two half-matrices, Spearman-Brown corrected [84]. (2) Self-identification: share of creators whose older half is nearest to its own newer half; chance is 1/66. (3) Agreement: Mantel correlation for the 6 pairs of representations, 9,999 creator-label permutations, raw value printed beside the value divided by the geometric mean of the two reliabilities. What embeddings add: partial Mantel of E on a reference matrix (same reviewed entity family, yes or no) controlling for B. (4) Clusters: average linkage and Leiden for k = 2 to 10; per-cluster Jaccard stability under resampling of recordings within creator (500 times) and the consensus co-assignment matrix [85][86]; a cluster is drawn only if its mean Jaccard is at least 0.75, otherwise the figure says "no stable partition". Reference for any structure: features permuted independently across creators (999). No pairwise tests.
- **Outputs.**
  - Fig SM-1: four square heat maps side by side in one row and column order (cluster order of B with optimal leaf ordering); lower triangle = similarity on a single-hue scale, quantile-scaled, raw range printed beneath; upper triangle = absolute split-half difference in grey; side strips for entity family, delivery-state mix, words and median recognizer confidence.
  - Fig SM-2: 4 x 4 heat map of Mantel correlations (raw and disattenuated printed), reliabilities on the diagonal.
  - Fig SM-3: mean cluster Jaccard against k, one line per representation, reference line at 0.75.
  - Fig SM-4: scatter of embedding distance against names-masked count distance, one dot per creator pair, coloured by same or different entity family.
  - Table SM-1: representation, tokens used, reliability, self-identification rate, chosen k or "no stable partition". Table SM-2: per creator the 5 nearest neighbours under each matrix and their ranks under the others; coded ids.
- **Pilot, exploratory (66 creators, random halves).** Self-identification 0.94; split-half matrix correlation 0.77 for frequent words and 0.85 for content words with names kept. Agreement between clusterings of the two halves (adjusted Rand) ran from -0.02 to 1.0 depending on k: distances reproduce, partitions do not.
- **Follows.** The noise ceiling [84]; cluster-wise stability [85]; consensus clustering [86]; sample size for style [55]; the uncontrolled similarity claim of [50]; language measures per community [15]. Sharpens plan figure A6-a and reuses the plan's cluster-sensitivity code.
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Random halves share intros, guests, adverts and neighbouring dates, so the pilot's reliabilities are overstated: halves are split by order after formulas and adverts are removed. Names and catchphrases identify a creator trivially: the masked variant leads. Similarity follows format and audio quality: the side strips show it, and the analysis is repeated with delivery-state shares and confidence partialled out. A dendrogram always shows clusters: the gate decides whether one is printed. Disattenuated values can exceed 1: the raw value is always beside them. Embedding models were trained on written text.

#### GB. Grounds for belief: four stance counts [value high; cost a day, plus list checks in HR]

- **Question.** On what grounds do speakers ask to be believed, and how do those grounds vary with format, topic and the presence of a forecast or an ask?
- **Data and unit.** Finished English transcripts from one recognizer model. The four counts are modelled separately, not as a composition. Co-occurrence as an estimate: the odds that a window with a booster also holds a hedge, against matched windows of the same recording (conditional logistic regression). Validation: 100 hits per list read in context by two readers (gate 0.8); 500 sentences rated for certainty on a 6-point scale by two readers against the list score; if that correlation is under 0.5 the lists are replaced by model-coded sentence certainty with a tuning set and a held-out set [18][46]. #### SV. Source or speaker: which carries the wording of a being [value high; cost days]

- **Question.** When several creators say they voice the same family of beings, how much of the variation in wording belongs to the family, and how much to the human speaker?
- **Data and unit.** English. The claimed source is taken from titles and descriptions first. In the titles of all 1,976 recordings: Pleiadian in 101 titles from 27 creators, named councils 51 from 13, Arcturian 46 from 17, Jesus or Christ 45 from 15, a galactic federation 37 from 8, archangels 34 from 13, Sirian 12 from 9. Seven families have 3 or more creators with 2 or more titled recordings each; 38 creators have one such family and 15 have two or more. Unit: creator-by-family cell with at least 2 recordings and 2,000 words, rarefied to equal tokens; inference over creators. Before SG's gate: whole recordings in which title and opening formula agree on the source. After it: the labelled spans.
- **Method.** The estimand is fixed beforehand: the family share of variance in wording, with its interval, judged against a smallest share of interest written into the registry (proposed: 0.05). All names of beings, stars and places and each creator's formulas are masked. (a) Similarity contrast on one primary representation, the function-word profile: mean similarity between cells of the same family and different creators, the same creator and different families, and different on both; references = family labels permuted across each creator's cells, and across creators within family size (9,999); differences with creator-bootstrap intervals and a leave-one-creator-out range per family. Content-word TF-IDF and window embeddings as sensitivity. (b) Variance split per recording-level feature (stance rates, speech rate, pause rate, ten topic log-ratios): crossed random effects for creator, family and creator-by-family, Bayesian fit with a half-t prior on each standard deviation [72], posterior share per component, and a table of the family share under three prior scales. (c) Shared core: terms used by at least 60% of the creators voicing a family and by under 20% of all others, each creator counted once [15][30]. (d) The contrast repeated after deleting each family's 100 most distinguishing words [54]. Validation: source label of 200 spans by two coders, "none or unclear" allowed.
- **Outputs.** Fig SV-1: paired dot plot, one dot per creator with two or more families, x = similarity to its own other-family cells, y = similarity to other creators' same-family cells, diagonal drawn, reference band shaded. Fig SV-2: stacked bars, one per feature, shares of variance for creator, family, creator-by-family and residual, credible intervals as whiskers, smallest share of interest marked. Fig SV-3: family-by-family heat map of mean cross-creator similarity, voicing creators in each axis label. Table SV-1: families by creators, recordings, words and tokens per cell. Table SV-2: shared-core terms per family with "n of N creators". Table SV-3: contrast before and after deleting distinguishing words, with reference percentiles. - **Follows.** One 2026 study of 3 channelers reported similarity of 0.65 to 0.79 for the same being across channelers and 0.75 to 0.88 within a channeler across beings, with no stated null [50] (summary page read, not the paper). Deep against superficial resemblance [54]; counting once per unit [15][30]; variance priors with few groups [72]; topic control [38].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Seven families are too few to estimate a variance: the prior table shows how much the answer depends on the prior. The informative contrast rests on 15 creators. Speaker dominance is near-certain beforehand, so the estimand is the family share, not which is larger. Similarity grows with cell size: equal tokens. A title names the being a recording is published under, not who speaks in a span. One prolific creator can define a family: equal weight per creator and leave-one-out. The text is neutral on whether beings exist; family labels follow the creators' own claims.

It can be reopened with two captures of the counts at a fixed age. The text says "title cue" and "delivery rate"; no creator ranking.

### 4.4 Calendar, outside events and language

#### EV. World events entering the recordings [value high; cost days; a feasibility gate comes first]

- **Question.** Which dated outside events of July to November 2026 are taken up, by what share of creators, and in which upload after the event?
- **Data and unit.** Recordings with a date interval of 7 days or less from the 20 ISO weeks of 2026 in which 20 or more creators posted (12 weeks have 30 or more creators). Event list: 20 dated events, about half scheduled and half unscheduled, each with a word list, plus 20 placebo word lists of matched frequency with no dated event. The lists are chosen, frozen and hashed by a person outside the project who has not read the transcripts or the crude counts. Gate per event: at least 15 creators with 2 recordings in the 4 weeks before and 2 in the 4 weeks after. Unit: creator-event-week; inference over creators.
- **Method.** Step 0: the feasibility table per event is published first; the experiment continues only if 8 or more events pass the gate. (a) Uptake: share of creators posting within 14 days after an event who mention it, Wilson interval, by content-axis third. (b) Stacked event study: mention (yes or no per creator-week, and rate per 1,000 words) on relative-week indicators from -4 to +4 with week -2 as the reference, creator and event random intercepts, a log-words offset, creator-bootstrap intervals; one code path and one reference shared with SY. Built-in falsification: unscheduled events must show no lead; a lead means a contaminated word list. The placebo lists give the reference band. (c) Speed: which upload after the event carries the creator's first mention (first, second, third, later, none), scheduled against unscheduled. (d) Deferred: the frame of each mention (delivery state, cause given, link to forecasts) is coded only after SG's gate and a double-coded sample of 300 mentions pass. Word-list precision from 50 hits per event read in context.
- **Outputs.** Table EV-0: feasibility per event (eligible creators, pass or fail). Fig EV-1: small multiples of the event study, one panel per event class, x = week relative to the event, y = change in the probability of a mention, band, placebo band in grey. Fig EV-2: dot plot with intervals, share of mentioning creators whose first mention came in the first, second or third upload after the event, scheduled against unscheduled. Fig EV-3: heat map, content-axis thirds by events in date order, cell = share of creators mentioning within 14 days. Table EV-1: per event, date, creators eligible, uptake share with interval, word-list precision. Table EV-2 (deferred): delivery state by cause given.
- **Crude counts, no longer blind for these topics.** Eclipse words in 13.6% of August recordings against 3.0 to 6.1% in September to November; AI words in 11.2% of recordings (46 creators); earthquake, volcano, hurricane or flood words 11.2% (46); comet or interstellar-object words 2.3% (18).
- **Follows.** Discussion after dramatic events [4]; attention shifts around dated events [8]; stacked events with leads and lags [29]; clusters matched to documented events [12]; chi-squared tables with Cramér's V [1].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Mentions are sparse: an event with 15 eligible creators may yield a handful of mentioning creators, hence the gate. Days to first mention would mostly measure how often a creator uploads (median gap 7 days): the upload ordinal replaces it. Some topics were already counted by month: the outsider's list restores blindness. Only about 20 weeks are dense and there is no earlier year: all contrasts are inside the corpus (R13). Explanations are reported as claims with no truth label, at group level, and no single creator's reading of a disaster is singled out (R15).

#### LG. Language case panel with a content arm [value medium; cost hours, plus reader checks per language]

Absorbs the coded inventory across languages. Serves roadmap phase 7d.

- **Question.** Where do the five non-English creators sit among the English creators on measures that are comparable across languages, and which content attributes survive the recognizer's translation?
- **Data and unit.** 5 non-English creators (Japanese 2; Spanish, Portuguese, Czech 1 each; 13 recordings finished at the last count, about 92 expected) against about 90 English creators. Unit: creator. Eight language-free measures: recording length, speech share, gaps of 1 second or more between segments per speech minute, long-pause time share, longest quiet stretch, slope of speech coverage over the recording, first-axis score of topic composition from multilingual sentence embeddings of original-language windows, and distinct names from the closed list found in the original text per speech hour.
- **Method.** No test of language (R11). (1) Each non-English creator's plain percentile among the English creators, per measure, with an interval from bootstrapping that creator's recordings; the floor is printed (no percentile-based p can fall below 1/91 = 0.011). (2) Predictive check: 95% prediction interval for a new creator with k recordings from VP's variance components; creators outside are marked. (3) Multiplicity stated, not corrected away: 5 creators x 8 measures = 40 placements, about 2 expected outside 95% by chance; that count is printed on the figure. (4) Only measures that BR shows to be bridge-safe are read; the rest are hatched. (5) Original against English view of the same recording: segment-count ratio, share of original speech time covered by an English segment, repeated-segment loops, names recovered in each view, as paired counts. (6) Content arm: at most 10 yes-or-no items with enough prevalence (families of beings, cosmology items, practices, world claims, an ask present, a dated forecast present), coded from the original-language transcript and reported only for a language where a human reader has checked 20 recordings; per-item agreement between the original and the English view (kappa with creator-bootstrap interval); nearest English creators by Jaccard distance on the item profile; write-in terms allowed and reviewed by a reader who knows the tradition before anything is called distinctive.
- **Outputs.** Fig LG-1: case panel, one row per measure, x = English-creator SD units, grey dots = English creators, 5 marked points with their own intervals, 95% prediction band shaded. Fig LG-2: heat map, 5 creators by 8 measures, cell = percentile among English creators (diverging palette centred at 50, value printed), hatched where not bridge-safe. Fig LG-3: paired slope chart per non-English recording, original against English view (segments, loops, names). Fig LG-4: content heat map, each of the 5 creators followed by its nearest English creators, by up to 10 items, cell = share of recordings with the item; human-checked languages only. Table LG-1: creator, language, recordings, hours, measure, value, percentile with interval, floor. Table LG-2: per item, agreement between original and English view, failing items flagged. Table LG-3: write-in markers with the reviewer's decision. Every caption carries: "language here means these five creators".
- **Follows.** Few clusters [68][71]; multilingual sentence embeddings [42]; translation evidence for formal written text only [41] and its effect on lexical richness [43]; thresholds validated per language [11]; review of items by a scholar of another tradition [25]; validation of language tools [9].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Language, creator, recognizer model and translation are one bundle: nothing here separates them. Words per minute, lexical diversity and anything computed on the translated text are excluded. Sub-word tokens make short pauses non-comparable in Japanese: only segment gaps of 1 second or more are used. The translate task loops and invents filler and replaces names: names are taken from the original. The five creators were not chosen to represent their languages, and two Japanese creators are two cases, not a group. Model coding of the original text is the least checked step: it is reported only with a human reader.

#### SY. Calendar synchrony across creators [value medium; cost a day]

Three panel members proposed this experiment; this is the merged version (SY, the shared-clock proposal, and panel C of visual F11).

- **Question.** Do creators' recordings become more alike around the same calendar dates, beyond what each creator's own habits and posting dates give, once the date words themselves are removed?
- **Data and unit.** English-original recordings with an exact upload day in 2026 (701 from 75 creators at the pilot; 10,181 cross-creator pairs within 3 days and 86,879 pairs 14 to 60 days apart). Creators need a span of at least 30 days. Event list fixed from an astronomical almanac before any text is read: the 2026 eclipses, equinoxes and solstices, the repeated-digit dates and 31 October. No lunar phases. The calendar lexicon is frozen in advance. Units: creator for intervals, recording pair for similarity, creator-week for the event study.
- **Method.** (A) Positive control for the dating pipeline, not a finding: the calendar-lexicon rate per 1,000 words against signed days to the nearest listed event (-21 to +21 in 3-day bins), creator-balanced mean with a simultaneous band; reference = 2,000 circular shifts of the whole date list by a random offset, which keeps its spacing; and a within-event contrast of the 7 days before against the 7 days after, within creators present on both sides. If the curve does not peak at the listed dates, the dating is wrong. (B) The claim: TF-IDF cosine for cross-creator pairs with names masked, each creator's formulas and platform boilerplate removed, and the calendar lexicon, date tokens and month names removed. S = mean similarity of pairs 3 days or less apart minus pairs 14 to 60 days apart. Reference: upload dates permuted among each creator's own recordings (9,999), which keeps every creator's vocabulary and posting counts and breaks alignment between creators. Interval by creator bootstrap. Event study on pair-centred similarity: for each pair of creators present in a week, cosine minus that pair's mean over all weeks they share; weeks -3 to +2 around each date, stacked over dates, only pairs seen in baseline weeks. (C) Leave-one-creator-out and leave-one-week-out ranges; S at day thresholds 1, 3, 7 and 14. (D) Use of the date: each mention coded as event logistics or sale, portal or energy day, forecast horizon, numerology, astrology report or other; 300 mentions double-coded. 11 November and the August eclipse were already seen and are exploratory. A registered version of S belongs to the replication wave.
- **Outputs.** Fig SY-1: event-time plot per event class (eclipse; equinox or solstice; repeated-digit date), x = days from the event, y = lexicon rate, band, shifted-list band in grey. Fig SY-2: histogram of the shifted-list statistic with the observed value marked. Fig SY-3: heat map, rows = coded creators with 8 or more dated 2026 recordings, columns = ISO weeks 31 to 47, cell = lexicon rate (grey = no upload), event weeks marked on the top axis. Fig SY-4: S against the day threshold with the permutation band. Fig SY-5: event-study plot, x = week relative to the date (-3 to +2), y = centred cross-creator similarity, one thin line per date, thick pooled line with band. Fig SY-6: stacked bars per date, share of mentions in each of the six use classes, mentions and creators printed. Table SY-1: event class, events, recordings within 3 days, creators, statistic, reference percentile. Table SY-2: S, interval, permutation p, leave-one-out ranges. Table SY-3: per date, creators before, after and both, registered or exploratory.
- **Feasibility counts.** Creators with a recording in the 7 days before / after / both: 11 November 59 / 52 / 44; 31 October 40 / 48 / 33; 10 October 36 / 36 / 24. 11 ISO weeks of 2026 hold 20 or more creators. Calendar-lexicon words occur in 27.5% of English recordings (59 creators).
- **Follows.** Event-time formats [4][5][8]; event-study plots with leads and lags [6]; date-shuffle and token-swap nulls [13][14].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** With a lunar phase every 14.8 days no day lies far from every event, and only about 15 days from July to mid-November lie 10 days from every listed date: placebo dates are replaced by shifting the whole list and by the before-against-after contrast. Calendar words rise near calendar dates by construction: part A is a control. The weeks holding the October and November dates also hold the most creators: similarity is centred within pairs. A creator whose 20 uploads fall in one week has almost no distinct permutations: the 30-day span. Similarity near an event shows shared attention, not borrowing, and no coordination between named creators is claimed. The commerce link to dates waits for AK's labels.

#### FA. First-appearance table for rare coined terms and dated forecasts [value low; cost a day]

The proposal's creator-by-creator lead matrix, flow diagram and narrative-cluster version are dropped for this cohort (section 6).

- **Question.** For rare coined terms and for dated forecasts that several creators share, who used each earlier inside the window, described at group or coded-creator level?
- **Data and unit.** Recordings whose upload-date interval is 7 days or less; creators with at least 5 such recordings in the 120 days before the capture (66 creators, 954 recordings in the date table). Items: coined terms and names after merging spelling variants, used by at least 3 creators with no creator holding over 60% of uses; dated forecasts that share a target event (from FL). Unit: creator.
- **Method.** Each creator's first use inside the window (midpoint of the date interval). "Used earlier" needs non-overlapping date intervals, a gap of 2 days or more, and both recordings inside the calendar span the two creators share. First share = items a creator used first / items it used, against a reference from shuffling upload dates among each creator's own recordings (1,000 times). Before any run: plant a leader with a known lead in the real upload calendar; if the reference does not detect it with 80% power, the table is published as description only. Items tied to a listed outside event or calendar date are flagged "common cause". Hand check: 100 earlier-use pairs read by two coders.
- **Outputs.** Table FA-1: the most shared items with an analyst-written label (no quotes), number of creators, first-use week, common-cause flag. Table FA-2 (only if the power check passes): by content-axis third or coded creator, items used, first share, reference mean, percentile. Table FA-3: result of the power check. Fig FA-1: histogram of delays in days for observed earlier-use pairs over the reference distribution.
- **Follows.** First-appearance tables [11]; shuffle nulls with a minimum lead [13]; lead scores against a null and a naming threshold [14]; originated topics [10]; adoption of new vocabulary [58].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** First use inside a 120-day window is left-truncated: a storyline a creator has used for years counts from its first use in the window, so the table says "used earlier in the window", never "first" or "origin" (R10). First in this corpus is not origin: older books and creators outside the roster are unseen. A news event moves creators together without contact. The text never says "copied".

### 4.5 Extending the sample (each needs the owner's decision)

#### RW. Replication wave [value high; cost new data] (added)

- **Question.** Do the registered results hold on recordings that no pilot has seen?
- **Data and unit.** For each of the 101 creators, the recordings uploaded after this cohort's capture, collected on one later date fixed now, up to 20 per creator, through the same pipeline and one recognizer model. Together with the 23 hold-out creators this is the confirmatory sample. Unit: creator.
- **Method.** The frozen registry (R2) is run unchanged, Holm across it, with the interval procedures verified in CA. A result replicates if its interval excludes zero in the registered direction and the estimate lies inside the pilot's prediction interval. Coverage of the wave by posting rate is reported, with a reweighted estimate as a sensitivity check.
- **Outputs.** Table RW-1: hypothesis, estimand, procedure, pilot estimate, hold-out estimate, wave estimate, Holm p, replicated yes or no. Fig RW-1: forest plot of the registered contrasts, pilot, hold-out and wave side by side. Table RW-2: creators and recordings in the wave by posting rate.
- **Follows.** Labelled analytic choices and multiverse reporting [28][73]; the plan's pre-registered families.
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Prolific creators fill the wave quickly and occasional posters not at all: Table RW-2 shows it. The wave is later in calendar time, so outside events differ: registered contrasts are within-recording or within-creator wherever possible.

#### CS. Comparison set of non-channelling spoken content [value high; cost new data] (added)

- **Question.** Which measures differ between this cohort and comparable spoken content that makes no channelling claim?
- **Data and unit.** About 20 creators in three strata (sermons, wellness talks, interviews), the newest 20 recordings each, same pipeline, same recognizer model. Roster, strata and selection rule are written down before any text is read. Unit: creator.
- **Method.** Each cohort measure (delivery, position contrasts, rhythm, stance counts, ask classes) estimated in both sets. Differences as standardised effects with intervals from the procedure verified in CA for 20 against about 100 creators, judged against an equivalence margin stated beforehand, so that "no difference" can be a finding. The registry may hold at most 2 comparison hypotheses. Until CS exists nothing is called distinctive of channelling (R13).
- **Outputs.** Fig CS-1: forest plot, standardised difference per measure with interval and equivalence margin. Fig CS-2: the comparison creators as marked points on the cohort distributions. Table CS-1: strata, creators, recordings, hours, words. Table CS-2: measure, difference, interval, verdict (different, equivalent, undetermined).
- **Follows.** Sermon and wellness-creator studies as templates [30][31]; the podcast corpus [26].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** The choice of comparison creators drives the result: the selection rule is fixed first and results are shown per stratum. Twenty creators detect only large differences on measures with a low creator share: CA states the detectable size.

#### SL. Selection sensitivity [value medium; cost hours for stage 1, new data for stage 2] (added)

- **Question.** How much do cohort numbers depend on taking the newest 20 per creator?
- **Data and unit.** Stage 1: the cohort as it is. Stage 2: an "earlier 20" sample for about 15 creators (seeded draw stratified by window span): the 20 recordings before the current newest 20. Unit: creator.
- **Method.** Stage 1: every headline cohort value under three weightings: equal per creator; weighted by the inverse of the window span; restricted to recordings dated in a common window of 120 days. Differences with creator-bootstrap intervals against a margin stated beforehand. Stage 2: paired difference newest minus earlier per creator, pooled by random-effects meta-analysis. This describes stability; it is not a trend.
- **Outputs.** Fig SL-1: per headline measure, three estimates with intervals on one scale. Table SL-1: the same numbers with margins. Fig SL-2 (stage 2): paired dots per creator, newest against earlier, with the pooled difference. Table SL-2: creators, spans, recordings.
- **Follows.** Sampling skew in platform datasets [22]; the quota-sample point of the ICWSM study.
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** Span weighting leans on occasional posters, whose dates are more often estimates: exact-day dates are used where possible and both versions are shown. Earlier recordings differ in age and platform conditions. Fifteen creators support description only (R10).

### 4.6 Internal checking

#### PF. One-page internal profile per creator [value medium; cost days; built last]

- **Question.** What does one creator's recent output look like in delivery, content mix, posting pattern and data limits, set against the cohort, and does the page agree with what a reader saw?
- **Data and unit.** Tables from T1, EB, RH, PO, CX and SM. Unit: creator. A full page needs 8 or more transcribed recordings; 5 to 7 carries a "thin data" banner; fewer gives no page.
- **Method.** No new statistics and no tests. Percentile positions with intervals from bootstrapping the creator's recordings, cohort held fixed. Identical layout and axis ranges on every page; the cohort in grey behind every mark. Two checks: for the 39 creators read by hand, each point where the page contradicts the reading notes is listed; two people who read transcripts match 10 pages with hidden ids to 10 creators, which shows that pages are identifiable, not that they are right.
- **Outputs.** One landscape page. Header: id, language, recognizer model, recordings selected and transcribed, other text kinds, hours, words, date window, badges. Row 1: 20 bars in listing order (height = log minutes, fill = non-speech share, outline = status); six percentile bars with dot and interval (pace, pauses, fillers, contractions, second-person rate, non-speech share). Row 2: the creator's speech-rate ridge over the cohort ridge; the delivery plane with 20 dots on the grey cohort; a 6 x 20 position strip. Row 3: term families as dots with intervals on the log-ratio scale; the 5 nearest creators by vocabulary and by delivery; a 52-week upload strip. Row 4: top 10 names with share of recordings and a hand-verified mark; count of dated statements by checkability once the ledger exists. Footer: what cannot be said from this page. Table PF-1: contradictions between pages and reading notes. Internal only.
- **Follows.** The reporting template of a creator-level study [31]; a map of channels with a quality score per group [2].
- **Pitfalls and safeguards.** A page about a named person reads as a verdict and inherits every unvalidated label on it: it is built last, after T1, EB, RH and PO pass their gates, and stays internal. No public per-creator page exists until a naming threshold, a removal route and the owner's decision exist (R15).

Parts removed from experiments that were kept:

- **Choosing among 7 interval procedures by simulated coverage (CA).** Selects on Monte-Carlo noise; one procedure per estimand is fixed first.
- **Back-extrapolated "corrected" estimates from injected errors (QD).** Rests on injected errors resembling real ones, which is false for names.
- **Permutation ticks for "creator share above zero" (VP)** and **a p-value for "creators differ in topic mix" (CX).** Certain to reject; they add nothing.
- **Common-drift tests, slope plots and order heat maps as findings (SD).** Order is not time and the statistics were already seen.
- **Curve typologies from functional components (PO).** Forking paths on 83 curves; allowed only behind SM's stability gate.
- **Runs between pauses of 0.5 seconds, mixture likelihood-ratio tests, the dip test for two modes (RH).** The first sits on an instrument seam; the others treat dependent values as independent.
- **Lunar phases and placebo dates (SY).** No day is far from a lunar phase.
- **The commerce-by-date regression (SY)** and **the voice-of-the-ask arm (AK)** until labels pass their gates.
- **A case t statistic on shrunken means (LG).** Plain percentiles say the same with fewer assumptions.
- **Four of eight stance lists and the stance-against-axis test (GB).** Rare lists drive log-ratios; the test was circular.
- **The creator-by-creator lead matrix, flow diagram and narrative-cluster version (FA).** A lead matrix reads as who copies whom; the window is left-truncated; the correction could pass only if 26 or more pairs hit the smallest attainable p together.
- **The view-count arm (TP).** Metadata unopened; views depend on age.
- **The cross-experiment correlation matrix of creator scores (from the tradition map).** About 190 correlations among derived scores that share transcript error. Any pair the owner wants must be named beforehand and take a place in the registry.
- **Public exemplars of timeline strips and public per-creator pages (SG, PF).** Not before the owner decides and ids are removed.

Gaps the challenger listed: six became experiments (HR, BR, SP, CS, RW, SL); negative-control outcomes went into CA and the missingness comparison into T1.

From the literature studies, not taken up:

- **Interrupted time series, difference in differences, regression discontinuity.** They need many observations on both sides of a date; they belong to the long archives.
- **Hawkes processes and transfer entropy between creators.** Too sparse at 20 recordings per creator.
- **Word-list emotion scores.** They did not track reported emotion on speech [39].
- **A dynamicity axis over recording order** [15]. It reads order as time (R10). Distinctiveness alone is covered by SM and SV.

## 7. Order of work

**Step 0: before any pending transcript is analysed.**

1. Freeze the registry (R2), at most 10 hypotheses in total. The 10 candidates named in this catalogue: up to 4 position contrasts (PO); the frame-to-main shift in contractions (SG, if the gate passes); the within-creator association of checkability and commitment, and relayed against embodied checkability (FL); same-days similarity S (SY, on the replication wave); agreement of the content-axis inputs (CX); the family share of variance against its smallest share of interest (SV). If the comparison set is collected, its 2 hypotheses take 2 of these 10 places. The owner decides the final list.
2. Fix the hold-out: the 23 creators that had at most 3 finished transcripts and 10 or more recordings still to come at the snapshot (366 recordings to come; 18 creators and 286 recordings not flagged non-English). They are a late part of the run, not a random draw, and CA must state what 18 creators can confirm. When this catalogue was written (1,331 finished transcripts) none of their pending recordings had been transcribed, so the hold-out was intact. For these creators only counts are computed until the freeze. Seven further creators also had at most 3 run transcripts, but most of their recordings sit in other text kinds; they become usable only after BR's pass.
3. Hash the lexicons, name lists, term families, event and date lists and thresholds. Commit Table CA-1 with the fixed procedures.
4. Draw the HR samples with recorded seeds and name the owner of the hand-work.

**The day the transcripts are complete (stored fields only, small compute, no labels).** T1 rerun with the missingness table. The artefact filter and QD stage 1. VP. SD. EB for delivery measures. PO on the seen creators as description. RH's instrument check, ridges and plane. LX. SL stage 1. The feasibility tables of EV and SY and SY's positive control. CX stage A for term families, with name-based columns flagged until recall is measured. LG rerun with the "not bridged" label. TP's title-cue counts.

**Needs the frozen corpus and a free machine.** CA (simulation, after the transcription worker has ended). The registered contrasts of PO on the hold-out creators. SM. CX stage B, once the topic model has run with 5 seeds. SY part B. FA with its power check. SV at recording level. BR and the pass over the 214 other-text recordings (GPU; owner's approval).

**Needs model labelling and human checks.** HR first, because QD stage 2, SG, RH's typology, GB, FL, AK, the name columns of CX and the content arm of LG all wait for it. Then SG's validation and gate, which releases every contrast by delivery state: within-format shrinkage in EB, the voice arms of FL and AK, the frame contrast for hedges, the span version of SV, the being cue of TP, the frame table of EV. SP runs beside it. PF is built last.

**Needs new data and the owner's decision.** RW (capture date fixed now), CS, SL stage 2.

Dependencies in one line each: VP feeds EB, CA and LG. SD feeds EB, CA and the ordered splits of VP and SM. HR feeds QD, SG, RH, LX, GB, FL, AK and CX. SG's gate feeds EB, GB, FL, AK, SV, TP and EV. CX supplies the groups used by EV, FL, GB and FA. BR decides which measures LG may read.
