# 14. What is new, and a candidate registry of hypotheses (proposal of 2026-11-20)

**Status.** This section is a proposal. The registry is frozen only when the owner approves it. Nothing in it has been tested, and no transcript of a hold-out creator has been analysed for it. Every number marked "proposed" is for the owner to confirm. The section fills the registry that rule R2 of the experiment catalogue (section 13) left empty, and it uses the labels of section 2.1 (pre-registered, conditional, registered exploratory, exploratory, gate). It contains no transcript text and names no creator, channel or being.

## 14.0 Basis and limits

**How it was made.** Two proposers, one working from social theory and one from speech and computation, wrote 24 proposals against the catalogue's 28 experiments and 16 rules. A skeptic judged each one for interest and testability and re-checked its novelty: 10 go to the registry with changes, 13 stay exploratory, and 1 was merged as a duplicate. This text applies the skeptic's changes. Where the writer added a detail that neither the proposals nor the verdicts contain, the text says "writer's addition".

**Search basis.** All novelty statements rest on metadata searches in free scholarly indexes made on 2026-11-20:

- first proposer: 96 searches (54 OpenAlex, 31 Crossref, 11 arXiv), the top 5 to 8 results inspected each; about 30 further requests were refused by rate limits after the free OpenAlex budget ran out;
- second proposer: counts are given per item below; its OpenAlex quota also ran out part-way, and it read abstracts for 13 works only;
- skeptic: 147 keyword searches (99 Crossref, 48 arXiv) and 30 DOI lookups; OpenAlex refused all 3 of its requests; it read abstracts for about 20 works;
- for this text: 84 Crossref DOI lookups and 7 arXiv identifier lookups, which confirmed the metadata of every work cited here. No new keyword search was made.

Crossref keyword search leans on titles. "Not found in N searches" therefore means only that, never "does not exist", and no statement here is a claim to be first. The reference list at the end says, for each work, whether an abstract was available to this text or only a title.

**Terms.** *Seen creators*: the 71 creators with five or more transcripts at the snapshot; everything computed on them is exploratory. *Hold-out creators*: the 23 creators the run reached last (18 of them not flagged non-English); they are a late part of the run, not a random draw. *Replication wave* (RW): what each creator uploads after the listing of 19 November 2026, collected on one later date (proposed: 20 February 2027). *Comparison set* (CS): creators who make no channelling claim (sermons, wellness talks, interviews). *Claimed voice*: speech that a creator presents as a channelled or relayed voice; within it, *embodied* (the being speaks in the first person) and *relayed* (the creator reports in their own voice what a being says). *Own voice*: the creator speaking as themselves. These follow the creators' own framing; the text takes no position on whether any being speaks.

**Two kinds of confirmation.** The hold-out gives out-of-sample evidence at creator level, from at most 18 English creators. The replication wave holds later recordings of the same roster: it shows that a pattern persists in these creators, not that it holds for new ones. Each entry says which of the two it gets. The roster is purposive, so no result describes channelers at large.

**Conditions every entry carries.** The catalogue's rules R1 to R16 apply: the creator is the unit; English recordings from one recognizer model; the artefact filter is applied first and results are shown with and without it; no word list or label is used before two people have measured it; Holm correction runs over the registry places that are run (the thresholds below assume all ten, the strictest case: 0.005 at the first step); every permutation or shift reference has enough draws for its smallest p to pass that step; claims of no difference use two one-sided tests against the stated margin; public outputs are numbers only.

**What must be frozen together with the registry.** Only H3, with gate E1, can be run on the hold-out creators without new human labels. The other places wait for labels, lists or new data. Three shared instruments have to be fixed, with a budget, in the same act as the registry; without the first two, five of the ten places (H1, H4 and H6; H5 and H9) are promises:

1. *One span-label protocol* (H1, H4, H6). Two people mark claimed-voice spans from the explicit verbal framing in the transcript text (announcement, self-naming, leave-taking), with all timing hidden, so that tempo cannot leak into the labels; kappa at least 0.6. They work from text, not audio. The catalogue's segmentation (SG) cuts recordings on rate and pauses, so its states cannot label a timing contrast (the contrast would be built in), and they should not label the contraction contrast either, because contraction is in part a phonetic reduction that plausibly follows tempo [mackenzie2013variation]. The protocol needs a stated budget beyond the 60 recordings of the hand-reference study (HR), on hold-out and wave recordings.
2. *One title-label rule* (H5, H8, H9): recordings titled as channelled or relayed against own-voice talks, and the family of beings a title names; frozen from metadata only and checked by two coders on a title sample (proposed: precision at least 0.9 on 100 titles for the voice label; 300 titles double-coded with kappa at least 0.6 for the family label).
3. *Lists and dates fixed before the wave is collected* (H2, H7, H10): the forecast ledger with its hash, the rule that excludes calendar and event-logistics dates, the almanac date list, the holiday-week rule, the calendar lexicon and the capture date.

## 14.1 What is new

Five statements. Each uses only claims the skeptic let stand, and each is a statement about what the searches returned.

**N1. A spoken corpus of channelled content across many speakers.** 101 creators, the newest 20 public recordings each, 1,946 archived with verified audio, about 746 hours transcribed with word time stamps, on a purposive roster. Closest prior work found: a laboratory study of 13 trance channels [wahbeh2019physiological]; elicited interviews with three channelers [wahbeh2026semantic]; 52 channelled submissions set against an archive [wahbeh2026correspondence]; qualitative studies of talk between mediums and sitters [wooffitt2001raising, bongelli2020demonstrative]; and transcript corpora of other genres: conspiracy videos [liaw2023younicon], religious videos [chen2025digital], podcasts [litterer2024mapping] and a text corpus of sermons [boussalis2021political]. A multi-speaker corpus study of channelled speech was not found in the first proposer's 96 searches, in the second proposer's 18 (8 OpenAlex, 6 Crossref, 4 arXiv) or in 14 further searches by the skeptic.

**N2. The claimed voice compared with the speaker's own voice, and the claimed source compared with the speaker.** Inside natural recordings and across many creators, the project compares what a creator presents as a being's voice with the same creator's own speech (H1, H4, H6), and tests with a stated reference distribution whether wording follows the family of beings a creator names or the creator (H9). Closest found: a laboratory task in which 13 people read one story in both states [wahbeh2019physiological]; a neuroimaging study of trance writing [peres2012neuroimaging] (title only; content as the skeptic recalled it); ethnography and conversation analysis [hughes1991blending, wooffitt2001raising]; and, for source against speaker, a comparison of three channelers [wahbeh2026semantic], known to the project only from its stated design, and a comparison of submissions with an archive whose abstract reports similarity scores and no reference distribution [wahbeh2026correspondence]. A comparison of a claimed voice with the same speaker's own voice in natural recordings was not found in 28 searches by the skeptic (delivery and register), nor in the proposers' searches (8 OpenAlex, 6 Crossref and 4 arXiv on delivery; 6 OpenAlex and 10 Crossref on register; 4 by the first proposer). A controlled test across many creators of source against speaker was not found in 6 Crossref searches by the skeptic (one OpenAlex request refused) or in 2 by the first proposer.

**N3. A ledger of dated forecasts that is fixed before the dates pass.** Statements about the world's future are coded for checkability and commitment in two blind passes (H2), and the dated ones are frozen in a hashed ledger before the replication wave, so that what a creator later says about a passed date is observed prospectively, with an undated control from the same creator (H10). Closest found: work on failed prophecy that begins after the failure, as theory [dawson1999prophecy, zygmunt1972prophecies], archival critique [kelly2026debunking], a survey of case studies [kelly2025failed] and ethnography of one group [tumminia2005prophecy]; and one computational study of self-disclosed dissonance in conspiracy forums, which does not follow forecasts [phadke2021characterizing]. A prospective ledger with follow-up across many speakers was not found in 12 searches by the skeptic or in 8 by the first proposer; both project digests report the same gap.

**N4. Two questions from the sociology of religion put as creator-level tests: who voices the ask, and whether a milieu without an organisation keeps common time.** An ask to buy or enrol is attributed to a voice inside the recording (H6), and similarity between creators near shared calendar dates is tested against dates permuted within each creator, with date words removed (H7). Closest found: taxonomies of creator income at channel or video level [hua2022characterizing, ballard2022conspiracy]; qualitative work on the boundary between the sacred and the commercial and on charisma [barcan2010spiritual, joosse2014becoming]; phrase tracking in the news cycle [leskovec2009memetracking]. Attribution of an ask to a voice was not found in 10 searches by the skeptic (4 by the first proposer); a synchrony test of this kind among independent spiritual creators was not found in 10 searches by the skeptic (6 by the first proposer).

**N5. Measurement and design, claimed as documentation and as application.** (a) The recognizer's invented text is located by its position in the fixed 30-second decoding window, and the planned filter removes it by position and not by wording, in a genre where silence and music are part of the content and the inserted phrase is one the genre itself uses. That such models invent text where audio holds no voice, and that a small set of strings recurs, is prior work [koenecke2024careless, baranski2025investigation]; long-form decoding in windows is known to drift and repeat [bain2023whisperx]; see also [wang2025calmwhisper, gandhi2023distilwhisper]. In the abstracts of the papers that 9 arXiv searches by the skeptic returned (5 arXiv and 1 Crossref by the second proposer), none reports where in a fixed window the invented text falls. The grid belongs to this pipeline's non-overlapping windows, not to the recognizer family; the non-speech share has not been checked by listening and the filter's precision and recall are unmeasured, so the right word is "documented". (b) Confirmation is reserved for a hold-out of whole speakers and a dated replication wave, under a registry capped at ten. Split samples and registration of text analysis are established [egami2022make, fafchamps2017using, anderson2017splitsample, vandenakker2021preregistration, vanmiltenburg2021preregistering]; what is claimed is their application at speaker level in a speech corpus. The design was fixed before the confirmation data were analysed, not before all contact with the data.

### H1. The claimed voice is slower: a field test of a laboratory report

- **Origin.** Second proposer's H1, with the slowing arm of the first proposer's H2. Skeptic: registry with changes (interest 4, testable 3).
- **Claim and direction.** Within a recording that holds both, spans a creator presents as a claimed voice are delivered more slowly than the same creator's own-voice spans: the pooled creator-level rate ratio (claimed to own) is below 1.
- **Why either outcome matters.** Yes: a difference in voice reported for 13 people reading one story in a laboratory extends to ordinary recordings across many creators, and the corpus supplies a field effect size with a prediction interval for a new creator. No (ratio inside 0.95 to 1.05): the slowing belongs to the reading task or to a visible minority style and not to the claimed voice in general. The outcome is open: a minority of fast claimed voices is expected, and relayed messages are reported not to change delivery, so the pooled effect could be near zero while single creators slow strongly. This is a field generalisation, not a replication: the laboratory task was reading a set text, and here speech is spontaneous.
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Unit: creator. Per creator, the mean over eligible recordings of log(rate in claimed-voice spans / rate in own-voice spans); rate is estimated syllables per minute of span time, pauses inside the span included and the gap at the boundary excluded; spans of at least 300 words each; own-voice spans exclude interviews until speakers are separated (SP). Creators are pooled by random-effects meta-analysis, with a 95% interval and a 95% prediction interval for a new creator. Reference: exact sign-flip of creator means. Verdicts fixed beforehand: *slower* if the upper bound of the ratio is below 0.95; *no slowing* if the interval lies inside 0.95 to 1.05; otherwise *not determined*. Negative control: the same contrast in recordings where the creator passes from a greeting to an own-voice main part. Named secondaries, estimates only: embodied and relayed spans separately; the split of any slowing into pause and articulation parts (E4).
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed).** 5% slower, a log ratio of -0.05.
- **Carried by.** SG's paired contrast with RH's timing features, on the span labels of 14.0 and not on SG's timing-cut states.
- **Waits for.** (1) The reading times of the laboratory paper checked in its full text: the abstract fetched for this text reports voice arousal and spectral-power differences and does not mention reading time, and the times in the project's digest were extracted by machine. Until then the word "replication" is not used, and the proposal's band of 0.55 to 0.68 for a "laboratory-sized" effect is dropped. (2) The span-label protocol and its budget. (3) The artefact filter. (4) The syllable estimator checked on HR excerpts, with words and letters per minute as sensitivity. (5) The wave.
- **Refuted by.** A pooled interval inside 0.95 to 1.05. Writer's addition, for the owner to confirm: if the negative control slows as much as the claimed voice (the interval of the difference between the two contrasts includes zero), the result is worded as slowing between frame and main part and not as a property of the claimed voice.
- **Confirmed on.** Hold-out creators and replication wave pooled, creator as the unit, with the hold-out-only estimate printed beside it. "Not run" below 12 creators with both voices: counting signs only, 8 creators must all agree for the smallest p (2^-8 = 0.0039) to pass the first Holm step, and 12 are needed to tolerate one dissenting creator (13/4096 = 0.0032). The skeptic estimates, without a count, that fewer than 12 of the 18 English hold-out creators publish both voices in one recording.
- **Closest prior work.** [wahbeh2019physiological] (abstract read); laboratory work on prayer [niebuhr2019god] and on hypnotherapy speech [grataloup2026sound]; guided-meditation delivery [niebuhr2026calm]; between- and within-speaker tempo variation [quene2005modeling] (the last four: titles only). A field study of rate in a claimed voice against the same speaker's own voice was not found in 12 Crossref keyword searches by the skeptic, nor in the second proposer's 8 OpenAlex, 6 Crossref and 4 arXiv searches (the arXiv searches returned nothing).

### H2. Checkable forecasts are hedged

- **Origin.** First proposer's H7; a candidate already named in the catalogue. Skeptic: registry with changes (interest 4, testable 3).
- **Claim and direction.** Within creators, the more checkable a statement about the world's future is (a count of five parts: time window, place, named actor, observable outcome, quantity), the less often it is stated with full commitment: the within-creator Somers' D between checkability and commitment is below zero, at fixed statement length.
- **Why either outcome matters.** Negative: the risk of disconfirmation is managed in the wording at the moment of utterance, before any date passes, which the failed-prophecy literature cannot see because it starts after the failure. Positive: specific forecasts are delivered with more force, the speaker staking authority on what can be checked. Null: hedging is a habit unrelated to checkability. The trade-off is known in judgment research; what is new is the genre and the within-speaker estimand, so this is the extension of a known trade-off and not a new phenomenon.
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Unit: creator with at least 30 validated world-future statements. Primary outcome, binary and coded on the main clause: bare or boosted against hedged or conditional. D is computed within statement-length bands and pooled, then pooled over creators by random-effects meta-analysis. Reference: commitment codes permuted within creator and within length band (9,999); creator-bootstrap interval; leave-one-creator-out range. The test is two-sided, because a positive D is a finding. Reported beside it: D within each band; the time-window part alone, apart from the five-part count; the four-step order (hedged, conditional, bare, boosted) as a sensitivity analysis. Calendar and event-logistics dates are excluded from the ledger by a rule fixed beforehand. The two dimensions are coded in separate passes, each blind to the other.
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed).** |D| of at least 0.10, the default the plan already uses for its hedging test B4.
- **Carried by.** FL.
- **Waits for.** FL's validation (600 sentences double-coded, kappa per part at least 0.6); HR's accuracy for spoken years and dates; the length bands and the exclusion rule, frozen.
- **Refuted by.** A 90% interval of D inside -0.10 to +0.10 (no trade-off), or a positive D whose interval excludes zero.
- **Confirmed on.** Hold-out creators and replication wave pooled, with the hold-out estimate shown apart; a margin of 0.10 cannot be resolved on the hold-out alone. "Not run" below 8 creators with 30 validated statements.
- **Closest prior work.** Laboratory judgments [yaniv1995graininess]; managers' choice of forecast specificity [bamber1998discretionary] (title only; content as the skeptic recalled it); strategic ambiguity [eisenberg1984ambiguity] (title only); forecasting skill from text [zong2020measuring]; certainty in science communication [pei2021measuring]; for prophecy, [dawson1999prophecy]. A quantitative test of specificity against commitment in prophetic or channelled speech was not found in 7 Crossref and 5 arXiv keyword searches by the skeptic or in 5 searches by the first proposer.
- **Stays exploratory.** The proposal's secondary, whether marking a source of knowledge (reporting clauses, perception verbs) [hu2022quotatives] goes with more specific statements, and the catalogue's candidate "relayed against embodied checkability".

### H3. Self-reference recedes inside the recording

- **Origin.** First proposer's H1. Skeptic: registry with changes (interest 3, testable 5).
- **Claim and direction.** In English recordings of ten minutes or more, with the first and last 180 seconds removed, the first-person singular rate is higher in the first and last tenth of the remaining running time than in its middle six tenths (trimmed contrast U above zero), each recording measured against its own mean.
- **Why either outcome matters.** Yes: self-reference recedes in the main part of a recording even after teasers, adverts and sign-off formulas are cut away. No: the pilot curve was the intro and outro of any talk video, or belonged to creators already seen. Without the trim the claim is close to trivially true, which is why the trimmed contrast is the registered one. Whether a positive U belongs to the genre or to the platform is H8's question; until H8 supports it, the result text does not use the words "ritual" or "bracket".
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Unit: creator (the mean over its eligible recordings; one recognizer model; artefact filter on; publisher-feed audio excluded; bins of at least 50 words). U is the log-ratio to the recording's own mean rate in the first and last tenth minus the same in the middle six tenths; the estimand is the creator-balanced mean of U. Reference: circular shift of each recording's bin sequence by a random offset (at least 999 draws, so the smallest p of 0.001 can pass the first Holm step); interval by the creator-bootstrap procedure fixed in the coverage audit (CA). The untrimmed U is reported, not tested. Named secondary, which needs only title labels: U in recordings titled as channelled minus U in the same creator's own-voice recordings. Writer's addition: the length and word minimums (ten minutes, 800 words) are applied to the recording after the trim.
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed).** U of at least 0.10 log units (edges about 10% above the middle); "negligible" if the 90% interval lies inside -0.10 to +0.10. The value should be checked against the trimmed contrast on the seen creators, which is exploratory and may be computed before the freeze.
- **Carried by.** PO, as one of its at most four registered contrasts.
- **Waits for.** The registry freeze and the artefact filter only; no hand labels. CA prints the number of hold-out creators with enough long recordings and the detectable U before the test is run.
- **Refuted by.** A hold-out U whose 90% interval lies inside -0.10 to +0.10, or a negative U.
- **Confirmed on.** Hold-out creators, on the day the registry is frozen; repeated on the wave.
- **Disclosure.** The pilot (exploratory, untrimmed, 10 bins) gave 34.3 first-person singular forms per 1,000 words in the first tenth against 25 to 27 in tenths 3 to 8. It drew on 83 creators, more than the 71 called seen, so early files of some hold-out creators probably entered it.
- **Closest prior work.** Position-normalised function-word curves in written stories [boyd2020narrative]; trajectories over transcripts of public talks [tanveer2018awe] (title only); openings and closings of vlogs as a described platform convention [jalagat2022welcome] (abstract read: ten vlogs); the concepts of footing [goffman1979footing] and of religious language [keane1997religious]. A position curve of speaker self-reference in religious or spiritual recordings was not found in 14 Crossref and 9 arXiv keyword searches by the skeptic or in 4 searches by the first proposer.

### H4. The claimed voice is a formal register: fewer contractions

- **Origin.** Second proposer's H4, which absorbs the contraction arm of the first proposer's H2; SG's candidate in the catalogue. Skeptic: registry with changes (interest 3, testable 3).
- **Claim and direction.** Within a recording, the share of contractible forms that are contracted is lower in embodied claimed-voice spans than in the creator's own-voice spans, and the pooled drop is at least half the standard deviation of own-voice contraction share between creators.
- **Why either outcome matters.** Yes: one speaker's switch into the claimed voice moves a core marker of informal speech about as far as speakers differ from one another, and unrelated creators make the same turn, which marks a convention of the genre. No: the claimed voice is marked by content and names and not by register, and the "formal trance voice" is the style of a visible minority. The direction is expected from decades of qualitative description, so only three things can change a reader's mind: the size against the yardstick, the prediction interval for a new creator, and the difference between embodied and relayed spans.
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Unit: creator. Within-recording difference, claimed voice minus own voice, in contraction share = contracted forms / (contracted + full forms) over a closed list of contractible pairs; the creator mean is divided by the between-creator standard deviation of own-voice contraction share, fixed on the seen creators before the freeze. Pooled by random-effects meta-analysis with a 95% prediction interval. Reference: sign-flip of creator means. Primary on embodied first-person spans; relayed spans as an estimate. Negative control: contraction share in fast against slow own-voice windows of the same creators, which shows how far contraction follows tempo alone. Reported beside it, not registered: the shift in log(first-person plural / first-person singular) and in hedge rate.
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed).** 0.5 between-creator standard deviations.
- **Carried by.** SG's paired contrast, on the span labels of 14.0.
- **Waits for.** The span-label protocol; an HR check that hand and recognizer transcripts give the same contraction share in each voice (the recognizer may normalise contractions); the closed list of pairs and the yardstick, frozen. Open point: the yardstick needs own-voice text of seen creators, so either seen creators are labelled by the same protocol or it is taken from recordings titled as own-voice talks; the owner chooses, and the choice is frozen.
- **Refuted by.** A standardised drop whose interval lies below 0.5; a positive shift; or a sign that differs across creators so that the prediction interval is centred on zero.
- **Confirmed on.** Hold-out creators and replication wave pooled, hold-out-only estimate beside it; "not run" below 12 creators, as for H1.
- **Closest prior work.** Formality as a set of dimensions of communicative events [irvine1979formality] and the marked use of language in religious settings, where the source of the words is itself in question [keane1997religious] (abstracts read); performance and its special codes [bauman1990poetics] (title only); ethnography of trance channelling [hughes1991blending] (title only); reported speech in sittings with mediums [wooffitt2001raising] (abstract read); trance against control for writing [peres2012neuroimaging] (title only); contraction as a variable with a morphological and a phonetic stage [mackenzie2013variation] (abstract read). A quantified within-speaker register shift for a claimed voice in speech was not found in 13 Crossref and 3 arXiv keyword searches by the skeptic or in 6 OpenAlex and 10 Crossref searches by the second proposer.

### H5. World claims sit in the claimed voice, practice in the speaker's own

- **Origin.** First proposer's H4. Skeptic: registry with changes (interest 4, testable 2); the strongest content hypothesis, with a measure that is not yet the construct.
- **Claim and direction.** Within a creator, vocabulary of claims about the cosmos and the world is denser, relative to vocabulary of practice and feeling, in recordings titled as channelled or relayed than in the same creator's own-voice talks (difference of log-ratios above zero).
- **Why either outcome matters.** Yes: channelling works as a licence for claims; statements about the cosmos, the past and the future are placed in the being's mouth, and advice on feeling and practice is given on the speaker's own authority. No (zero or negative): the claimed voice mostly delivers reassurance and practice and the doctrine is carried by the human as lecturer. Either answer changes whether the genre is better read as revelation or as therapy. The outcome is open: in crude counts therapeutic vocabulary dominates (soul and frequency terms each in 61% of English transcripts, any extraterrestrial word in 19%).
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Unit: creator with at least 2 recordings titled as channelled or relayed and at least 2 solo own-voice talks; body text only (the middle 80% of running time); guided meditations, promotions and interviews excluded from both sides. Per creator: log(world-claim rate / practice rate) in the channelled recordings minus the same in the own-voice talks; rates per 1,000 words with a half-count correction and a minimum number of hits on each side, so that the correction does not carry the estimate (proposed: 10 hits per group and side). Pooled by random-effects meta-analysis; reference: sign-flip over creators (9,999); creator-bootstrap interval.
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed).** 0.4 log units: the odds of world-claim against practice vocabulary 1.5 times higher in the claimed voice.
- **Carried by.** CX's term families and GB, read by voice.
- **Waits for.** The two word groups rebuilt before the freeze. As proposed, the world-claim side (contact and disclosure, agencies, ancient-astronaut terms, prediction horizons) measures extraterrestrial-disclosure vocabulary, so a creator on the devotional side has near-zero counts in both voices; frequency and vibration terms sit on the practice side although in this milieu they are also claims about the world; and guided meditations, own-voice and therapeutic by design, would produce the predicted sign by construction. Required: a world-claim group that covers every lineage, the frequency and vibration family split or removed, two-coder precision of at least 0.8 per group, or instead a sentence sample coded as world claim against practice instruction. Also: the title-label rule; labels for the excluded kinds of recording; the wave.
- **Refuted by.** A 90% interval inside -0.4 to +0.4 (no division of labour between the voices), or a negative difference whose interval excludes zero.
- **Confirmed on.** Hold-out creators and replication wave pooled; "not run" below 12 creators. Few creators publish both kinds: 4 of the 34 longer recordings read were own-voice talks.
- **Known rival.** Own-voice talks and channelled sessions are made for different occasions, so the difference is voice plus occasion. The result is worded accordingly.
- **Closest prior work.** How esoteric movements legitimise knowledge claims [hammer2001claiming] (title only); qualitative themes of channelled content [anastasia2020qualitative, wahbeh2026qualitative] (titles only); an ethnography of channelling, seen here only as a review record (doi 10.2307/3712358); narrative and persuasion coding of religious videos [chen2025digital]. A within-speaker contrast between what is said in a claimed voice and in the speaker's own was not found in 8 Crossref keyword searches by the skeptic, in its 14 searches for corpora of the genre, or in 3 searches by the first proposer.

### H6. The claimed voice does not sell

- **Origin.** First proposer's H5. Skeptic: registry with changes (interest 4, testable 2); it takes the one place for asks in preference to E10.
- **Claim and direction.** Within a recording, asks to buy or enrol occur at a lower rate per 1,000 words in claimed-voice spans than in own-voice spans (rate ratio below 1).
- **Why either outcome matters.** Yes: commerce is kept out of the claimed voice and the human handles the money, as accounts of charisma and of boundary work between the sacred and the commercial predict. Keeping the offer in the own-voice frame is the mechanism by which this happens, not a confound to remove. No (ratio near or above 1): the claimed voice itself sells, so its authority is spent directly on the transaction, which matters for how the genre's economy is described. The reading of 42 transcripts did not settle which holds. The result describes where asks are placed and says nothing about intent.
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Unit: creator. Within-recording rate ratio of validated purchase-or-enrolment asks, claimed-voice spans against own-voice spans, in the recordings labelled for H1; third-party adverts removed; creator-level log rate ratio with a half-count correction; reference: sign-flip over creators; creator-bootstrap interval. Span labels and ask labels are made in separate passes, each blind to the other. Reported beside it: the share of creators whose claimed voice voices at least one purchase ask, with an interval; the position-adjusted ratio as a sensitivity analysis; practice asks, with no registered direction; the confusion matrix of the span labels, which bounds misattribution.
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed).** Ratio of 0.5 or lower for "does not sell"; equivalence band 0.67 to 1.5 for "sells like the human".
- **Carried by.** AK, arm (e), on the span labels of 14.0.
- **Waits for.** Ask labels validated on HR's shared sample (kappa at least 0.6, F1 at least 0.6); the span-label protocol.
- **Refuted by.** An interval inside 0.67 to 1.5, or a ratio above 1.
- **Confirmed on.** Hold-out creators and replication wave pooled; "not run" below 12 creators with both voices. Asks are sparse and label error pulls the ratio toward 1.
- **Closest prior work.** A taxonomy of off-platform monetisation detected at video level [hua2022characterizing] (abstract read); monetisation of conspiracy videos [ballard2022conspiracy]; boundary work of spiritual healers [barcan2010spiritual] (title only); the social construction of charisma [joosse2014becoming] (abstract read). ### H7. A shared calendar produces shared content

- **Origin.** First proposer's H10; SY's candidate in the catalogue. Skeptic: registry with changes (interest 3, testable 3), kept as the one calendar hypothesis.
- **Claim and direction.** After date words, names and each creator's formulas are removed, the excess similarity of recordings by different creators uploaded within three days of each other, over pairs 14 to 60 days apart, is larger in weeks that hold an almanac-listed date than in other weeks.
- **Why either outcome matters.** Yes: a milieu with no organisation, clergy or liturgy still keeps common time, and shared calendar moments produce shared content. No: the dates are labels on otherwise independent content, which fits a market of separate brands better than a community of practice. That near pairs are more alike than far pairs at all is close to certain, from shared news and season, so that contrast (S) is reported without a test and only the listed-date excess is registered.
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Unit: creator; the measurement is on pairs of recordings from different creators. S is the mean TF-IDF cosine of cross-creator pairs at most 3 days apart minus pairs 14 to 60 days apart, centred within creator pair, with names masked and the calendar lexicon, date tokens and month names removed. Estimand: S in listed-date weeks minus S in other weeks, with the weeks of civil and religious holidays excluded or treated as their own class by a rule and a date list fixed before collection. Reference: upload dates permuted among each creator's own recordings (9,999); creator-bootstrap interval; leave-one-creator-out range; a leave-one-date-out range is mandatory. Positive control, not a finding: the calendar-lexicon rate must peak at the listed dates. Negative control, description only: S among comparison creators, if they are collected in the same calendar window.
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed).** To be stated in absolute cosine units from the pilot on seen creators, not as a share of another quantity. No pilot value of S exists yet. Placeholder for the owner: 0.01.
- **Carried by.** SY, parts A to C, on the replication wave.
- **Waits for.** The wave; exact-day upload dates; the lexicon, the almanac list and the holiday rule, frozen before collection; creators with a span of at least 30 days.
- **Refuted by.** An interval of the excess inside the margin or below zero; or an excess that disappears when one date is left out.
- **Confirmed on.** Replication wave. The window runs from late November to late February: it holds few listed dates, and the December solstice sits beside the year-end holidays, the New Year and the season of year-ahead forecasts. The result speaks for those dates only, and similarity near a date shows shared attention, never contact between creators.
- **Closest prior work.** Shared phrases in the news cycle [leskovec2009memetracking]; channel similarity on the platform [shajari2026characterizing] (title only); the sociology of calendars, seen here only as the review record of a 1981 book (doi 10.2307/2578250). A similarity test among independent spiritual creators with dates permuted within creator was not found in 7 Crossref and 3 arXiv keyword searches by the skeptic or in 6 searches by the first proposer.

### H8. The position contrast belongs to the genre, not to the platform

- **Origin.** First proposer's H12. Skeptic: registry with changes (interest 4, testable 2); the one comparison hypothesis worth a place.
- **Claim and direction.** The trimmed edge-against-middle contrast in first-person singular rate (the U of H3) is larger in cohort recordings titled as channelled than among comparison creators who preach or give wellness talks.
- **Why either outcome matters.** Yes: the receding self-reference is something channelling adds beyond the intro-and-outro shape of any talk video, and it becomes the first measure the project may call distinctive (R13). No (equivalent): the contrast is the platform's convention and H3 cannot be read as ritual. The sermon stratum is informative alone: preaching has a ritual frame, yet the preacher remains the author of the words.
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Unit: creator. Standardised difference in creator-level trimmed U between cohort creators (recordings titled as channelled, taken only from recordings no pilot has seen: hold-out creators and the wave) and comparison creators in the sermon and wellness strata. Interviews are shown apart, because two speakers give a different first-person curve. Interval by the two-group procedure that CA verifies; verdict by two one-sided tests against the margin, so that "no difference" can be a finding.
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed), and what it needs.** With 20 comparison creators against about 100 the standard error of a standardised difference is about 0.245 and a 90% interval has a half-width of about 0.40, so equivalence inside 0.5 is reachable only if the estimate lies within about 0.1 of zero, and a true difference of 0.5 has well under 80% power before Holm. Two ways out: enlarge the comparison set to about 40 creators and keep a margin of 0.5 standard deviations (half-width about 0.31), or keep 20 and widen the margin to about 0.8 and say so. Proposed: the first. Writer's note: the primary contrast uses two of the three strata, so with about 20 creators in three strata it would rest on about 13 (half-width about 0.48); the enlargement should be counted in the sermon and wellness strata. The detectable size is printed before any text is read.
- **Carried by.** CS with PO's contrast.
- **Waits for.** The comparison roster and selection rule, frozen before any text is read; the same pipeline and one recognizer model; the artefact filter; the title-label rule; the wave.
- **Refuted by.** An interval inside the margin (equivalent), or a larger contrast among comparison creators.
- **Confirmed on.** Comparison set. The choice of comparison creators drives the result, so strata are shown separately.
- **Closest prior work.** Religious videos coded for narrative and persuasion [chen2025digital]; a podcast corpus as a general spoken baseline [litterer2024mapping]; a text corpus of more than 110,000 sermons [boussalis2021political] (abstract read); vlog openings and closings [jalagat2022welcome]; position curves [boyd2020narrative]. Channelled recordings set against other religious or wellness speech on a within-recording contrast were not found in the skeptic's 23 position and structure searches plus 2 on sermon corpora, or in 4 searches by the first proposer.

### H9. One family of beings, many speakers: shared teaching, not shared voice

- **Origin.** First proposer's H9, which absorbs the second proposer's H6 (timing, now E8) and H7 (phrase reuse, now E9); SV's candidate in the catalogue. Skeptic: registry with changes (interest 3, testable 2).
- **Claim and direction.** Among creators who say they voice the same family of beings, the same-family excess in similarity is larger for names-masked content vocabulary than for function-word style.
- **Why either outcome matters.** Yes: what circulates under a family's name is a teaching, a lexicon and themes, and not a way of speaking; each speaker gives the being their own voice. No, with a style excess as large as the content excess: conventional voices attach to named families and are reproduced by speakers who have never met, which would point to performance itself being passed on through recordings. Neither contrast above its reference: the family name is a label only. The test repeats, with controls and a stated reference, a published comparison of three channelers.
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Unit: creator-by-family cell (at least 2 recordings and 2,000 words, rarefied to equal tokens; whole recordings in which title and opening formula agree on the family); inference over creators. For each representation, the contrast is the mean similarity of cells with the same family and different creators minus that of cells with different families and different creators. One estimand is registered: the contrast for names-masked content-word TF-IDF minus the contrast for the function-word profile, with all names of beings, stars and places and each creator's formulas masked. Reference: family labels permuted across creators within family size (9,999); creator-bootstrap interval; leave-one-creator-out range per family. It is also run within one tradition, so that a difference in subject matter between traditions cannot carry it, and after deleting each family's 100 most distinguishing words. Writer's addition, for the owner to confirm: the two representations have different similarity scales, so each contrast is divided by the standard deviation of different-family, different-creator similarities in its own representation before the difference is taken. Named secondary: the 15 creators who voice two or more families (similarity to their own other-family cells against similarity to other creators' same-family cells). Description only: family shares of variance with the table of prior scales, because seven families cannot fix a variance; and the timing and phrase arms (E8, E9).
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed).** A difference of 0.2 in those standardised units. Placeholder: it replaces the catalogue's share of 0.05, which applied to variance shares, and should be checked on cells of seen creators before the freeze.
- **Carried by.** SV, parts (a) and (d).
- **Waits for.** The closed name list for masking; the family label of titles under the title-label rule.
- **Refuted by.** An interval of the difference at or below zero.
- **Confirmed on.** Each hold-out creator that carries a titled family against the seen creators, so that one side of every pair is unread; then the replication wave.
- **Limits.** Seven families have three or more creators with two or more titled recordings. A title names the being a recording is published under, not who speaks in a span.
- **Closest prior work.** A comparison of three channelers [wahbeh2026semantic]; 52 submissions against an archive, with similarity of 0.66 to 0.88 and no reference distribution in the abstract [wahbeh2026correspondence] (abstract read); stylometry described as largely unexplored for mediumistic writings [weibel2024mediumship] (abstract read); speaker attribution from transcripts under topic control [aggazzotti2023authorship]; character voices written by one author [vishnubhotla2019fictional] (title only); deep against superficial resemblance between discourses [stine2021using] (title only). A controlled test of this kind was not found in 6 Crossref keyword searches by the skeptic or in 2 by the first proposer.

### H10. Silence after the date (conditional)

- **Origin.** First proposer's H8. Skeptic: registry with changes, last place, conditional (interest 5, testable 1): the most novel idea in the set and the one most likely to end "not run".
- **Claim and direction.** Within creators, dated statements about the world's future whose target date has passed receive a later reference by the same creator less often than matched undated statements, over an equal follow-up window. The registered direction is negative; the test is two-sided.
- **Why either outcome matters.** Classic theory expects either renewed proselytising or a repertoire of rationalisations, and both assume that the speaker must answer for the prophecy. Yes: in a feed of recordings nobody is made to answer, and the forecast is simply not mentioned again. No: creators return to passed dates as often as to anything else, or more, and so behave like the bounded groups of the case literature. The control from the same creator separates silence about passed dates from ordinary turnover of topics.
- **Estimand, unit, reference distribution.** Prospective. Before the wave is collected, a ledger is frozen and hashed: time-anchored world-future statements from the cohort whose target window ends between the listing date and 30 days before the wave's capture date (about nine weeks under the proposed date), each paired with an undated world-future statement by the same creator from the same or an adjacent recording, matched on scope by a rule fixed beforehand. Unit: creator with at least one matured statement and two or more wave recordings. Estimand: within-creator risk difference, dated and matured minus undated control, in receiving any later reference in the wave (restated, re-dated, explained, claimed fulfilled, admitted). Reference: sign-flip over creators (9,999); creator-bootstrap interval. Two readers first resolve what a passage refers to and then code follow-up, blind to whether a statement is dated or control. No statement is scored true or false; "matured" means only that the date has passed. The plain share of matured statements that are followed up is reported as a lower bound, with no verdict on what is "the norm": live streams, short videos and other platforms are outside the data.
- **Smallest effect of interest (proposed).** A risk difference of 0.15 in either direction.
- **Run condition.** At least 50 matured statements from 15 creators (the catalogue's condition); otherwise "not run", with the counts. The crude pass found about 300 candidate sentences with a time window, of which an earlier pilot suggests about half are forecasts.
- **Carried by.** FL's accounting census on RW.
- **Waits for.** Validated time-window labels; HR's accuracy for dates; the capture date fixed; the ledger hashed.
- **Refuted by.** A risk-difference interval that lies wholly above -0.15 (no shortfall of interest), or a positive difference whose interval excludes zero.
- **Confirmed on.** Replication wave.
- **Closest prior work.** [dawson1999prophecy]; [zygmunt1972prophecies] (title only); [kelly2026debunking] and [kelly2025failed] (abstracts read); an ethnography of a contactee group with an unfulfilled landing date [tumminia2005prophecy] (abstract read); self-disclosed dissonance in conspiracy forums [phadke2021characterizing] (abstract read). A prospective ledger of dated forecasts with follow-up across many speakers was not found in 7 Crossref and 5 arXiv keyword searches by the skeptic or in 8 searches by the first proposer.

## 14.3 Exploratory questions

Thirteen proposals did not make the registry. Each keeps a fixed method and carries the EXPLORATORY badge of section 2.1; none takes a registry place.

**E1. Measurement gate: is the recognizer's inserted phrase tied to the decoding window or to silence?** (Second proposer's H10; label "gate (estimand)".) If the inserted two-word thanks phrase has a constant chance per 30-second window, its rate per word must rise as speech slows, and any per-word count of thanks or closing formulas is then tied to tempo and so to delivery state. That half is arithmetic. The empirical half is an equivalence test: within a recording, the chance of an insertion per window is the same in the slower and the faster half of its windows, within a factor of 1.25 (windows of at least 20 words, split at the recording's median articulation rate; insertions counted as the excess of exact-phrase segments in the last 3 seconds of a window over 3/27 of those earlier; halves permuted within recording, 9,999; final windows excluded). It decides whether the artefact filter can be positional or must also use silence, and it runs first, on the hold-out creators, from stored fields. It is a statement about this pipeline's fixed windows, not about the recognizer family. If the owner wants a confirmatory methods result, it can take the conditional place of H10. Prior work: [koenecke2024careless, baranski2025investigation, bain2023whisperx, wang2025calmwhisper, gandhi2023distilwhisper]; 9 arXiv and 1 Crossref search by the skeptic.

**E2. Does the genre outweigh the person?** (First proposer's H3; registered exploratory.) With names and formulas masked, is a creator's claimed-voice function-word profile more similar to other creators' claimed-voice profiles than to the same creator's own-voice profile? Estimand, simplified by the skeptic: the first similarity minus the second, creator-balanced, with own voice taken from solo talks only and the verdict rules written now. That differences of register show in function words as strongly as differences of author is a standing result in stylometry: one paper argues that stylometric differences between authors are differences of register [grieve2023register] (abstract read; see also [baayen1996outside], title only, content as the skeptic recalled it), and the project's digest reports that speaker attribution from transcripts drops once topic is controlled [aggazzotti2023authorship]. New here is only the setting. It can also pass without any claimed voice, since a greeting frame and an expository monologue differ in register for every speaker. It needs at least 1,500 labelled tokens per voice and creator, far beyond the label budget. Promoted by amendment if at least 12 creators qualify. Not found for a claimed voice in 4 Crossref searches by the skeptic.

**E3. Tempo and address over running time.** (Second proposer's H9, with the second-person contrast of the first proposer's H1; registered-exploratory siblings of H3 inside PO, trimmed by 180 seconds in the same way.) Is articulation rate higher at the edges of a recording than in its middle, and does second-person address rise from the first to the last tenth? Both were already seen in the pilot on 83 creators (pace ratios of 1.02, about 0.985 and 1.04; second-person forms 37.9 to 57.5 per 1,000 words). The edges hold teasers, adverts by other speakers and music, where time stamps are least reliable, and a positive result has no reading without labels or a comparison set. Nothing closer was found than [boyd2020narrative] for wording and [goldmaneisler1961significance] for articulation rate (4 Crossref and 2 arXiv searches by the skeptic).

**E4. Is the slowing carried by silence or by articulation?** (Second proposer's H2; named conditional secondary of H1, estimate and interval only.) If H1 shows at least 5% slowing, what share of it comes from a larger share of pause time and what share from slower articulation? The split is exact, since log rate = log articulation rate + log(1 - pause share). The expected answer restates a classic result, that articulation rate is close to a personal constant [goldmaneisler1961significance] (abstract read). The instrument is weak exactly here: time stamps come from the recognizer's own alignment, short pauses can be absorbed into word durations, the voice detector leaves a seam at half a second, and a ratio is unstable when the slowing is small. An energy-based measure of voicing on the audio would be the proper instrument and needs an audio pass.

**E5. Silent against filled hesitation.** (Second proposer's H3; described beside H1.) Do long silent pauses rise while filler words fall in the claimed voice? More silence with almost no filled pauses is the documented profile of prepared oratory against interviews [duez1982silent] (abstract read), so by itself it says nothing about a claimed state. The measure also rests on the words the recognizer drops most ([smith2025prompting], title only), and 54 hand-transcribed excerpts cannot estimate deletion by voice precisely enough. It becomes testable only with the comparison set, sermons as the control, and verbatim hand transcripts.

**E6. Is the claimed voice more formulaic?** (Second proposer's H5; registered exploratory once the span labels exist.) Two accounts predict opposite signs: performers under real-time pressure draw on a stock of formulas ([kuiper1984livestock], abstract read; [rosenberg1970formulaic], title only), and planned discourse is said to be lexically more varied ([ochs1979planned], title only; the reading is the proposer's). Measure: LX's leave-one-recording-out formula coverage, by voice. After 150 words are trimmed at each end, own-voice spans rarely reach 300 words, so the number of creators who survive the length rule is reported first. Meditation scripts repeat by design; recognizer loops inflate repetition and recognition errors break exact runs.

**E7. Do opening and closing frames transfer to unseen creators?** (Second proposer's H8; registered exploratory, within-recording contrast only.) An inventory of 4-to-6-word runs found at the edges of recordings of the 71 seen creators is frozen and hashed before any hold-out transcript is read, and scored on the hold-out creators: coverage of their edges against matched windows from their middles, with a circular-shift reference. It is a clean transfer test and useful for segmentation, but an inventory chosen for sitting at edges will cover edges, and what is shared may be the platform's greeting and sign-off [jalagat2022welcome]; absolute coverage is reported, and, once comparison creators exist, the share of inventory runs that also occur there. Related: formulas in spontaneous sermons [rosenberg1970formulaic] and a chapter on the oral formula in the performed sermon (doi 10.9783/9781512801637-005, a record without an author). 3 Crossref searches by the skeptic.

**E8. Does a claimed source carry a tempo?** (Second proposer's H6; timing arm of H9, description only.) Articulation rate and pause share are added to SV's feature list. No test: an upper bound below 0.05 on a family's share of variance cannot be established from seven families, and speaker dominance in tempo is close to certain beforehand (the creator carries 0.58 of the variance of pace in the pilot; see also [quene2005modeling], title only).

**E9. Does phrasing travel with a claimed source?** (Second proposer's H7; shared-core arm of H9, exploratory count.) With names masked and each creator's own formulas removed, does a creator share distinctive 5-to-8-word runs with same-family creators more often than with others? Exact runs rarely survive automatic transcription, so counts will be sparse and biased toward no difference, and a family's self-identification formula can survive name masking; an edge-trimmed run is the sensitivity check. Its design, each hold-out creator against the seen creators, is taken over by H9. Related: [leskovec2009memetracking], [wilkerson2015tracing], [wahbeh2026correspondence].

**E10. Do asks follow urgency?** (First proposer's H6; reserve.) Within a recording, is an ask more likely in the five minutes after a statement of threat than after a control point at the same position? The design is good (position-matched controls, and the odds before the anchor must be lower than after it). Two changes are needed so that the coupling is not true by construction: anchors are statements of threat about the world, never offer deadlines; and the primary asks are purchase or enrolment and off-platform moves, with preparedness reported apart. Anchors are rare and two new label sets are needed. Related: a meta-analysis of fear-appeal experiments [tannenbaum2015appealing] (title only) and [ballard2022conspiracy]. A time-ordered within-recording test in spoken content was not found in the searches shared with H6.

**E11. Does conspiracy vocabulary follow the claimed lineage?** (First proposer's H11; description with intervals inside CX.) Do creators whose titles name extraterrestrial collectives use secret-control vocabulary in more of their recordings than creators whose titles name angelic or devotional figures, while consciousness-shift vocabulary is equally common in both? It is a between-creator difference on a purposive roster, near-obvious in direction, with groups defined from titles that cover part of the roster; and the wave would not confirm it independently, since it holds later recordings of the same creators. Only the equivalence arm is informative. Related: [ward2011emergence], [asprem2015conspirituality] (title only), an interview study of creators of such content [de2025whoever] (abstract read). 4 Crossref and 1 arXiv search by the skeptic.

**E12. Do dated titles deliver dated statements?** (Second proposer's H11; an estimate, no test.) The delivery rate, the share of recordings with a time cue in the title that contain a time-anchored statement about the world's future, is reported with its interval once the forecast labels pass validation. The rise over a creator's other recordings is near-certain, the rate depends wholly on labels that an earlier pilot found right about 56% of the time, and few creators have three cued and three uncued titles. Related: mismatch between title and content on the platform [zannettou2018good], [varshney2021unified] (titles only). 1 Crossref search by the skeptic, 3 by the second proposer.

**E13. Where do the non-English creators sit on language-free timing?** (Second proposer's H12; a stated count in the language case panel, no test, R11.) On measures that the recognizer bridge (BR) shows to be safe, how many placements of the five non-English creators fall outside the 95% prediction interval for a new English creator? The proposal's threshold, 4 of 40, is arbitrary, and the 40 is the catalogue's count for eight measures; the proposal lists six timing measures, which would give 30, so the count must be restated for the measures actually used. Five creators, two recognizer models and a translation step are one bundle. Related: a multilingual study of silent-pause duration [campione2002largescale] (title only).

## 14.4 Rejected

Proposals and parts of proposals:

- The first proposer's H2 (formal and slow) as an entry of its own: a duplicate; its slowing arm is H1 and its contraction arm is H4.
- H1's verdict "a fraction of its laboratory size" (the band 0.55 to 0.68): reading time of a set story and spontaneous rate are not commensurable, and the laboratory times are unverified.
- The word "replication" for H1: the abstract of the laboratory paper does not state slower reading; it stays out until the full text is checked.
- Timing-cut delivery states as labels for H1, H4 and H6: the timing contrast would be built in, and contraction is not independent of tempo.
- The untrimmed edge-against-middle contrast as the test of H3: close to trivially true for any talk video and already seen on 83 creators; reported, not tested.
- The words "ritual" and "bracket" for H3: not before H8 supports them.
- A four-step commitment scale as the primary outcome of H2: hedged, conditional, bare and boosted are not one scale; kept as sensitivity.
- H2 without a control for statement length: longer statements carry more checkable parts and more room for hedges.
- H5's word groups as proposed: they measure extraterrestrial-disclosure vocabulary, misfile frequency and vibration terms, and let guided meditations produce the predicted sign.
- H6's position-stratified ratio as primary: the own-voice frame is the edge of the recording by definition, so the strata would be nearly empty; kept as sensitivity.
- The first half of H7 (near pairs more alike than far pairs) as a test: close to certain from shared news and season.
- A smallest effect for H7 stated as 5% of a similarity gap: it must be in absolute units from the pilot.
- H8 with 20 comparison creators and a margin of 0.5: it would most likely end "undetermined".
- A second comparison hypothesis (shared edge frames, cohort against comparison creators): an inventory learned from one genre covers that genre better almost by construction; the place stays empty.
- H9's variance-share verdicts (family share of style below 0.05; content share above style share): seven families cannot fix a variance, so the answer would follow the prior; and content differs between traditions for reasons of subject matter.
- H10's verdict that silence is "the norm" (follow-up share below one half): one to three months of follow-up on one platform can make it true by censoring alone.
- E10 with offer deadlines as anchors and preparedness among the primary asks: the coupling would be true by construction.
- E5's wording "a third profile": it is the documented profile of prepared oratory.
- Registry places for E11 (near-obvious; the wave is not independent) and for E1 as a finding about the recognizer family (half of it is arithmetic; the grid is this pipeline's).
- Tests for E8, E12 and E13: an equivalence that seven families cannot establish; a rate that depends wholly on unvalidated labels; five creators, described only.

Novelty wordings that did not stand:

- Any "first" or "no one has": replaced by "not found in N searches".
- Verification as a contribution: receipts with byte counts and digests are standard fixity practice in digital preservation [kunze2018bagit]; keeping the 30 failed downloads in the selection is good practice. Neither proposer checked whether the comparison corpora report audio-level verification.
- "Design fixed before reading": the catalogue was written after 42 transcripts had been read and crude counts run on the seen creators; the hold-out is the tail of the run, and some of its early files entered descriptive tables.
- A registry or split sample as a new design, and "no paper at the venue uses a registry": the methods are established, and the second statement is the project's digest of 32 papers speaking.
- "Measured" or "validated" for the non-speech share and the artefact filter: "documented".
- "These questions have so far been addressed only by ethnography and case study": conversation analysis, qualitative content analysis, a laboratory voice study and computational work on dissonance exist ([wooffitt2001raising], [bongelli2020demonstrative], [anastasia2020qualitative], [wahbeh2019physiological], [phadke2021characterizing]); the accurate wording is that no creator-level quantitative test was found.
- "Slower delivery in trance is the one published behavioural difference": unverified, as under H1.
- "Genre outweighs person is new", "a position curve in spoken recordings is new", "specificity against commitment has not been tested": each is a known result or method in another setting (E2, H3, H2).
- "Hypotheses that only timed speech can test": the time stamps are the recognizer's own alignment; timing claims finer than about one second are not supported until the hand-reference study says so.
- Calling both the hold-out and the wave "confirmation" without the distinction of 14.0.

1. Approve, change or cut the ten places, and set the date of the freeze, which must come before any transcript of a hold-out creator is analysed.

3. Check the reading times of the 2019 laboratory paper in its full text. The paper draft currently says that this study "reports slower reading" (related work) and speaks of "a published report of slower delivery in trance" (experimental setup); both sentences depend on that check.

7. H5: who rebuilds the two word groups, or whether a coded sentence sample replaces them.
8. Whether E1 stays a gate or takes the conditional place of H10.
