H3, tested and withdrawn

H3 asked whether “I”, “me” and “my” are denser at the start and end of a recording than in its middle, once the first and last three minutes are cut. On the 17 hold-out creators H3 passed its registered rule. That rule was not calibrated at creator level: its reference rotates the positions inside each recording and holds the creators fixed, and the estimate is not centred: the mean of that shift reference is −0.063, not 0. That value, recorded in registry amendment A9, was computed by the second-round panel's calibration check on the 61 creators seen earlier, not on the hold-out creators. A later check on the creators seen earlier found that the rule rejects more often than its nominal level when there is no effect, and that it has little power at the smallest effect of interest. The 90% interval over creators includes zero, so H3 is not determined at creator level; the owner withdrew it (amendment A6). The registered result stays here. With nothing cut the contrast is large among the creators seen earlier: the surplus sits in the opening and closing minutes. Beyond the registered test, no estimate by position in the recording is shown for the hold-out creators. Dot: estimate in log units. Line: 90% interval over creators.

Do the two recognizer models agree?

English was transcribed by an English-only model and the other languages by a larger one. 36 English recordings, one per creator, were transcribed by both. Each row is the larger model minus the English-only model, in units of the spread between creators, with a 90% interval. A measure whose interval lies inside the shaded band (0.2 on either side) can be compared across the two models; one whose interval lies wholly outside it cannot; the rest are not settled by 36 recordings. Neither transcript was checked by a person. The comparison is of means over 36 English recordings of 5 to 60 minutes. Single recordings differ more: for pace the limits of agreement (mean difference plus or minus 1.96 SD of the differences) are -0.51 to +0.46 of the spread between creators. Leaving out any one recording changes none of the 13 settled rows. Nothing here applies to other languages, where language and creator change with the model.

Recognizer artefacts

Share of the 1,284 English transcripts of the 71 creators seen before the freeze (finished run, English-only model) that hold each pattern. Window-phase counts attribute at least 94.9% of the stand-alone thanks phrases that start in the last 3 s of a window to the recognizer, but 83.7% of all stand-alone thanks phrases sit inside running speech and nobody has listened to the audio, so these are counts of a pattern, not of checked insertions. For the isolated segments the bound is weaker: window-phase counts tie at least 62.5% of them to the window. Nothing on this tab, nor the run's counts, distributions and speaking pace, has these artefacts removed; only the H3 test and the comparison of the two models applied the artefact filter.

How to read this page

The corpus is the newest 20 public recordings of each of 101 creators who say they channel non-human beings. A creator's newest 20 can cover anything from a few days to many years, so nothing here is a trend. Each language other than English comes from fewer than five creators, so the languages other than English are shown only as one pooled row. No row, bin or cell describes fewer than five creators: cells with fewer than five creators are pooled, and two counts of creators of nested groups never differ by one to four. Figures on the first five tabs describe the finished run: counts of the whole run come from its ledger, and timing measures from the creators seen before the registry freeze only. The last tab describes an earlier snapshot. The hypotheses of the first round are tested on the hold-out creators with labels made by language models, which no person has checked, so every verdict is model-coded; beyond the counts of the whole run, the hold-out creators enter only the registered tests.
