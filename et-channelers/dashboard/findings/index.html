Registered tests so far: H3, tested and dropped

H3 asked whether “I”, “me” and “my” are denser at the start and end of a recording than in its middle, once the first and last three minutes are cut. On the hold-out creators the registered test passed, but the interval over creators includes zero and the larger group seen earlier shows nothing, so the owner dropped it. With nothing cut the contrast is large in both groups: the surplus sits in the opening and closing minutes. Dot: estimate in log units. Line: 90% interval over creators.

Do the two recognizer models agree?

English was transcribed by an English-only model and the other languages by a larger one. 36 English recordings, one per creator, were transcribed by both. Each row is the larger model minus the English-only model, in units of the spread between creators, with a 90% interval. A measure whose interval lies inside the shaded band (0.2 on either side) can be compared across the two models; one whose interval lies wholly outside it cannot; the rest are not settled by 36 recordings. Neither transcript was checked by a person.

How to read this page

The corpus is the newest 20 public recordings of each of 101 creators who say they channel non-human beings. A creator's newest 20 can cover three days or fifteen years, so nothing here is a trend. Languages other than English come from a handful of creators each. Figures on the first five tabs describe the finished run; the last tab describes an earlier snapshot. The hypotheses will be tested with labels made by a language model, which no person has checked.
